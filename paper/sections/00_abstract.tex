\begin{abstract}
Crowd-model steering terms are credited, on outcome alone, with clearing deadlocks at narrow doors. In one social force model (wall range 0.08\,m) at a 0.8\,m door, two rest states hold the last agents. In the lone trap the door-edge wall force alone holds one agent, the blocker. The pair standoff, the deadlock at wall range 0.04\,m beside the door, needs segment ends sharper than the agents' overhang (\RevBfiveOverhangMin--\RevBfiveOverhangMax\,mm); rounding at $r_c=0.1$\,m removed all \RevBfiveN. Optimal reciprocal collision avoidance coupled as a force (force-coupled ORCA) frees the lone trap through its goal-directed part: added to the model's goal-directed drive, it gives the same model with relaxation time \RevAoneTauPrimeLo--\RevAoneTauPrimeHi\,s and \RevAoneDriveRatioLo--\RevAoneDriveRatioHi\ times the drive at rest. This part alone completes \RwOneDriveK\ of \RwOneN\ whole runs, against \RwOneOrcaK\ for force-coupled ORCA. As a velocity filter, ORCA frees none. Matched per-step noise frees deadlocks by fluctuations; a matched lateral bias tilts the pair standoff out of its minimum. Probes of saved deadlocks assign three mechanism classes and a defect flag; replay and the time step separate per-step noise from force-coupled ORCA. With the blocker alone, noise frees it by activated escape once the barrier exceeds 1.5 noise temperatures, with Arrhenius slope \RvAthreeRatioDeep--\RvAthreeRatioMid\ of the computed barrier, the transition-state value. A sign defect makes the authors' earlier ORCA solver replay-sensitive. In five CroMa experiments at this width all tracked participants pass; with uncalibrated defaults the model passes at most \RevDThreeRatioMin--\RevDThreeRatioMax\ of human flow, \RvBoneCroOffMin--\RvBoneCroOffMax\ without its density-dependent speed reduction.

\end{abstract}
