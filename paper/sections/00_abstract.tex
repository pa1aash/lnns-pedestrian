\begin{abstract}
Our calibrate-then-falsify protocol tests whether a steering paradigm credited with clearing a deadlock causes the clearing: it assigns each resolution claim a physically defined mechanism class, tunes a control lacking the paradigm's machinery to the paradigm's rate, and compares them on frozen deadlocks by signatures not used in the calibration. We apply it to optimal reciprocal collision avoidance (ORCA), added as a force to a social force model, at a 0.8\,m exit as the last agents evacuate. The two deadlock types are a lone trap set by the door-edge wall force and, under a short-range jamb wall law, a pair standoff. Three controls match ORCA's rate (noise on lone-trap freeing, noise on run completion, a lateral bias on pair freeing); each differs from ORCA in replay, time-step dependence, time to free or handedness, and the bias frees partly different standoffs. At the lone trap ORCA acts through its rest drive, noise through activated escape of the blocker over a barrier its neighbours lower. In the CroMa bottleneck rebuilt like for like, all model variants pass only \EtenCSigFlowRatio--\EtenCThreeFlowRatio\ of the human flow.

\end{abstract}
