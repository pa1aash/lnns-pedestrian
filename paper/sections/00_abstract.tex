\begin{abstract}
At a 0.8\,m exit the last agents of a social-force evacuation deadlock in two ways. A lone agent is held by the wall force at the door edges if and only if the peak backward force of the two edges, $g_{\max}(r)$, exceeds its drive $m v_0/\tau$; under the default wall law it brackets the width, \EsevenWcrit\,m, at which half the runs complete. Under a short-range wall law two agents form a pair standoff before the jamb faces, a strict minimum of the rest-state energy that a lateral bias removes non-smoothly where a member reaches the end of a jamb segment. Velocity noise frees the lone trap by activated escape. No contact arch persists. We then screen optimal reciprocal collision avoidance (ORCA), added as a force. Rate-matched surrogates reach ORCA's rate by different physics: ORCA through its rest drive, noise through activated escape, a lateral bias through a tilt of the pair minimum. Replay, time-step dependence, time to free, final state and handedness separate them, and replays expose a sign defect in the legacy ORCA solver. ORCA coupled through the velocity has no rest drive and frees no deadlock, as predicted. The open framework deadlock-screen implements the screening protocol. In the rebuilt CroMa bottleneck every model variant passes only \FixAsevenCSigFlowRatio--\FixAsevenCThreeFlowRatio\ of the human flow.

\end{abstract}
