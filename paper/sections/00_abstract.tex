\begin{abstract}
Steering terms added to crowd models are credited with how deadlocks at narrow doors form and clear. We test such credits in one social force model (wall range 0.08\,m) at a 0.8\,m door. Two rest states hold the last agents. In the lone trap the door-edge wall force alone holds one agent, the blocker; the pair standoff needs sharp segment ends. With a wall range of 0.04\,m at the jambs, \EsevenBjambK\ of \EsevenN\ runs complete and \EsevenBjambCollective\ of \EsevenBjambClassified\ deadlocks are pair or group standoffs. Optimal reciprocal collision avoidance (ORCA) added as a force (force-coupled ORCA) frees the lone trap through its rest drive: the drive plus its drive part is the model with relaxation time $\tau'=\RevAoneTauPrimeLo$--$\RevAoneTauPrimeHi$\,s and \RevAoneDriveRatioLo--\RevAoneDriveRatioHi\ times the rest drive. The ORCA velocity filter adds no rest drive and frees none. Noise fraction-matched to force-coupled ORCA frees deadlocks by fluctuations; a matched lateral bias tilts the pair standoff out of its minimum. Probes, implemented in the open framework deadlock-screen, assign three mechanism classes and a defect flag; replay and the time step separate noise from force-coupled ORCA. With the blocker alone at $\beta\ge1.5$, noise frees it by activated escape with an Arrhenius slope \RvAthreeRatioDeep--\RvAthreeRatioMid\ of the computed barrier, as transition-state theory predicts. In the authors' earlier solver, replays reveal outcome sensitivity that traces to a sign defect. In five CroMa runs at this width no participant deadlocks; with uncalibrated defaults, whole-run model flow is \RevDThreeRatioMin--\RevDThreeRatioMax\ of human flow.

\end{abstract}
