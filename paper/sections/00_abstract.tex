\begin{abstract}
Crowd simulations credit how often agents deadlock at narrow doors, and how deadlocks clear, to added steering terms. We test such credits in one social force model (wall range 0.08\,m) at a 0.8\,m door. Two rest states hold the last agents. The lone trap, a blocker held by the door-edge wall force alone, is set by the wall range. The pair standoff needs sharp segment ends; at a wall range of 0.04\,m, \EsevenBallK--\EsevenBjambK\ of \EsevenN\ runs complete and \EsevenBjambCollective\ of \EsevenBjambClassified\ deadlocks (jambs only) are pair or group standoffs. Optimal reciprocal collision avoidance (ORCA) added as a force (force-coupled ORCA) frees the lone trap through its rest drive: the drive plus its drive part is the model with relaxation time $\tau'=\tau\tau_{\rm orca}/(\tau+\tau_{\rm orca})$ and multiplies the rest drive by \RevAoneDriveRatioLo--\RevAoneDriveRatioHi. The ORCA velocity filter has no rest drive and frees no deadlock. Velocity noise fraction-matched to force-coupled ORCA frees deadlocks by fluctuations, and a matched lateral bias tilts the pair standoff out of its minimum. Probes, implemented in the open framework deadlock-screen, assign three mechanism classes and a defect flag; replay and the time step separate noise from force-coupled ORCA. With the blocker alone at $\beta\ge1.5$, noise frees it by activated escape over an effective barrier \RevBthreeAllDeep--\RevBthreeAllMid\ of the computed one. In the authors' earlier solver, replays reveal outcome sensitivity that traces to a sign defect. In the five CroMa runs at this width no participant deadlocks; with uncalibrated defaults, model flow is \RevDThreeRatioMin--\RevDThreeRatioMax\ of human flow.

\end{abstract}
