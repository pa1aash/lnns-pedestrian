\begin{abstract}
When adding a steering paradigm to a pedestrian model clears a deadlock that the base model keeps, is the paradigm the reason? A control that shares none of its machinery, tuned to reproduce its completion rate, shows only that a simpler cause would suffice. We propose a calibrate-then-falsify protocol: each resolution claim is assigned to a physically defined mechanism class, and the matched control is confronted with the paradigm on held-out signatures measured on a library of frozen deadlock states. In a social force model at a 0.8\,m exit, deadlocks fall into two classes: a lone agent held by the wall force at the door edges, and a symmetric pair standoff, a strict energy minimum that a lateral bias removes at a fold. Noise matched to ORCA's rate at the lone trap, noise matched to its completion of whole runs and a bias matched to it at the pair reproduce the rate, yet free different deadlocks and differ in replay, time-step dependence or handedness: ORCA adds a goal drive, noise drives activated escape over computed barriers. Compared like for like with the CroMa experiments, every model variant either deadlocks or flows at \EtenCSigFlowRatio--\EtenCThreeFlowRatio\ of the human rate.

\end{abstract}
