\begin{abstract}
Crowd-model steering terms are credited, on outcome alone, with clearing deadlocks at narrow doors. In one social force model (wall range 0.08\,m) at a 0.8\,m door, two rest states hold the last agents. In the lone trap the door-edge wall force alone holds one agent, the blocker. The pair standoff needs segment ends sharper than the members' overhang (\RevBfiveOverhangMin--\RevBfiveOverhangMax\,mm); rounding at $r_c=0.1$\,m removed all \RevBfiveN. With wall range 0.04\,m beside the door, \EsevenBjambK\ of \EsevenN\ runs complete and \EsevenBjambCollective\ of \EsevenBjambClassified\ deadlocks are standoffs. Optimal reciprocal collision avoidance coupled as a force (force-coupled ORCA) frees the lone trap through its goal-directed part; this part alone completes \RwOneDriveK\ of \RwOneN\ whole runs, against \RwOneOrcaK\ for force-coupled ORCA. As a velocity filter, ORCA frees none. Matched per-step noise frees deadlocks by fluctuations; a matched lateral bias tilts the pair standoff out of its minimum. Probes of saved deadlocks (open framework deadlock-screen) assign three mechanism classes and a defect flag; replay and the time step separate per-step noise from force-coupled ORCA. With the blocker alone, noise frees it by activated escape once the barrier exceeds 1.5 noise temperatures ($\beta\ge1.5$), with Arrhenius slope \RvAthreeRatioDeep--\RvAthreeRatioMid\ of the computed barrier, the transition-state value. The authors' earlier ORCA solver has a sign defect that makes outcomes replay-sensitive. In five CroMa experiments at this width all tracked participants pass; with uncalibrated defaults the model passes at most \RevDThreeRatioMin--\RevDThreeRatioMax\ of human flow, \RvBoneCroOffMin--\RvBoneCroOffMax\ without its density-dependent speed reduction.

\end{abstract}
