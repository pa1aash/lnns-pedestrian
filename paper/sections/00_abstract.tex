\begin{abstract}
Crowd simulations credit how often agents deadlock at narrow doors, and how the deadlocks clear, to the steering terms a model adds. We test such credits in one social force model with a wall range of 0.08\,m at a 0.8\,m door. Two rest states hold the last agents. The lone trap, a blocker held alone by the door-edge wall force, is set by the wall range; the pair standoff of two members needs sharp door-edge segment ends and a short wall range. Optimal reciprocal collision avoidance (ORCA) added as a force frees the lone trap through its rest drive: at rest this force-coupled ORCA equals the model with relaxation time $\tau'=\tau\tau_{\rm orca}/(\tau+\tau_{\rm orca})$, a drive \RevAoneDriveRatioLo--\RevAoneDriveRatioHi\ times larger. The ORCA velocity filter has no rest drive and frees no deadlock. Velocity noise and a lateral bias, each fraction-matched to force-coupled ORCA at a stated horizon, free deadlocks by other physics. Replay, time-step dependence, time to free and whole-run final states separate noise from force-coupled ORCA, and handedness the bias. Noise frees the lone trap by activated escape over an effective barrier \RevBthreeAllDeep--\RevBthreeAllMid\ of the computed one. Replays expose a sign defect in the legacy ORCA solver. The open framework deadlock-screen implements the screening protocol. In the CroMa experiments no participant deadlocks at this width, and the model passes \RevDThreeRatioMin--\RevDThreeRatioMax\ of the human flow.

\end{abstract}
