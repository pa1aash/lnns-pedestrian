\begin{abstract}
Crowd simulations credit how often agents deadlock at narrow doors, and how deadlocks clear, to added steering terms. We test such credits in one social force model (wall range 0.08\,m) at a 0.8\,m door. Two rest states hold the last agents. The lone trap, a blocker held by the door-edge wall force alone, is set by the wall range; the pair standoff needs sharp segment ends and dominates at a wall range of 0.04\,m. Optimal reciprocal collision avoidance (ORCA) added as a force (force-coupled ORCA) frees the lone trap through its rest drive: at rest its drive part is the model with relaxation time $\tau'=\tau\tau_{\rm orca}/(\tau+\tau_{\rm orca})$, which multiplies the rest drive by \RevAoneDriveRatioLo--\RevAoneDriveRatioHi. The ORCA velocity filter has no rest drive and frees no deadlock. Velocity noise fraction-matched to force-coupled ORCA at horizon $T$ (lone keystones, whole runs) frees deadlocks by fluctuations, and a matched lateral bias tilts the pair standoff out of its minimum. Replay and the time step separate noise from force-coupled ORCA by definition, and time to free and final state empirically; handedness separates the bias. With the blocker alone, noise frees it by activated escape over an effective barrier \RevBthreeAllDeep--\RevBthreeAllMid\ of the computed one. Replays expose a sign defect in ORCA's legacy linear-program solver. The open framework deadlock-screen implements the protocol. In the five CroMa runs at this width no participant deadlocks, and with uncalibrated default parameters model flow is \RevDThreeRatioMin--\RevDThreeRatioMax\ of human flow.

\end{abstract}
