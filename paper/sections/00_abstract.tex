\begin{abstract}
Crowd simulations credit how deadlocks at narrow doors form and clear to added steering terms. We test such credits in one social force model (wall range 0.08\,m) at a 0.8\,m door. Two rest states hold the last agents: the lone trap, a blocker held by the door-edge wall force alone and set by the wall range, and the pair standoff, which needs sharp segment ends. With a wall range of 0.04\,m at the jambs, \EsevenBjambK\ of \EsevenN\ runs complete and \EsevenBjambCollective\ of \EsevenBjambClassified\ deadlocks are pair or group standoffs. Optimal reciprocal collision avoidance (ORCA) added as a force (force-coupled ORCA) frees the lone trap through its rest drive: the drive plus ORCA's drive part is the model with relaxation time $\tau'=\tau\tau_{\rm orca}/(\tau+\tau_{\rm orca})$ and multiplies the rest drive by \RevAoneDriveRatioLo--\RevAoneDriveRatioHi. The ORCA velocity filter adds no rest drive and frees none. Noise fraction-matched to force-coupled ORCA frees deadlocks by fluctuations; a matched lateral bias tilts the pair standoff out of its minimum. Probes, implemented in the open framework deadlock-screen, assign three mechanism classes and a defect flag; replay and the time step separate noise from force-coupled ORCA. With the blocker alone at $\beta\ge1.5$, noise frees it by activated escape with an Arrhenius slope \RvAthreeRatioDeep--\RvAthreeRatioMid\ of the computed barrier, as transition-state theory predicts. In the authors' earlier solver, replays reveal outcome sensitivity that traces to a sign defect. In five CroMa runs at this width no participant deadlocks; with uncalibrated defaults, model flow is \RevDThreeRatioMin--\RevDThreeRatioMax\ of human flow.

\end{abstract}
