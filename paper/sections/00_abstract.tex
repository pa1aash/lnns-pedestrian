\begin{abstract}
Crowd-model steering terms are credited, on outcome alone, with clearing narrow-door deadlocks. In one social force model (wall range 0.08\,m) at a 0.8\,m door, two rest states hold the last agents. In the lone trap the door-edge wall force alone holds one agent, the blocker; half the runs complete at the critical width \RevBtwoWfifty\,m. In the pair standoff two agents stand abreast at sharp door edges; standoffs are the only deadlocks at wall range 0.04\,m beside the door (\EsevenBjambK\ of \EsevenN\ runs complete). Optimal reciprocal collision avoidance coupled as a force (force-coupled ORCA) frees the lone trap through its goal-directed part: added to the drive, it gives relaxation time \RevAoneTauPrimeLo--\RevAoneTauPrimeHi\,s and \RevAoneDriveRatioLo--\RevAoneDriveRatioHi\ times the drive at rest. This part alone completes \RwOneDriveK\ of \RwOneN\ whole runs, against \RwOneOrcaK\ for force-coupled ORCA. An ORCA force replacing the drive frees only the \RzTonezeroLoneFreed\ of \RzTonezeroLoneN\ lone keystones that this force balance predicts. As a velocity filter, ORCA frees no saved deadlock. Per-step noise matched in freed fraction acts by fluctuations; a matched lateral bias tilts the pair standoff out of its minimum. Replay and the time step separate per-step noise from force-coupled ORCA. With the blocker alone, noise frees it by activated escape above 1.5 noise temperatures, with Arrhenius slope \RzTtwoRatio\ of the computed barrier. A sign defect makes our earlier ORCA solver replay-sensitive. In five CroMa experiments at this width everyone passes; the uncalibrated model passes \RevDThreeRatioMin--\RevDThreeRatioMax\ of human flow, \RvBoneCroOffMin--\RvBoneCroOffMax\ without density-dependent speed reduction.

\end{abstract}
