\section{Related Work}\label{sec:background}
\subsubsection{Steering Models.}
The social force model relaxes each pedestrian towards a desired velocity under exponential repulsion from others and walls~\cite{helbing1995}, with body compression and sliding friction in contact~\cite{helbing2000nature}; force-based models oscillate under strong repulsion and overlap under weak~\cite{chraibi2010,lakoba2005}. In their unstable regime pedestrians move backwards, which Chraibi et al.\ trace to the model class~\cite{chraibi2015}. The time-to-collision (TTC) law makes the interaction energy fall off as $t_c^{-2}$ in the predicted time to collision $t_c$~\cite{karamouzas2014}. ORCA truncates a neighbour's velocity obstacle, the own velocities that collide at some future time~\cite{fiorini1998}, at a look-ahead time, shares the avoidance, reduces it to a half-plane and returns the permitted velocity nearest the preferred one~\cite{vandenberg2011}. Hybrid crowd models combine such velocity-based avoidance with external physical forces~\cite{kim2015}.

\subsubsection{Clogging at Bottlenecks.}
Grains, colloids, sheep and simulated pedestrians at a narrow outlet alternate between bursts and clogs, and are clogged when the mean stoppage duration diverges~\cite{zuriguel2014}. Bottleneck experiments measure pedestrian flow against width~\cite{hoogendoorn2005,kretz2006,seyfried2009}, faster-is-slower at constrictions~\cite{pastor2015} and clogging in competitive evacuations~\cite{garcimartin2017}. A granular clog is a frictional arch that vibration unjams~\cite{janda2009} by escape from traps with distributed depths~\cite{nicolas2018trap}. Noise can also jam: stronger random forces crystallise driven counterflowing particles (freezing by heating)~\cite{helbing2000freezing}. In a velocity-based pedestrian model, prolonged clogs depend on exit width, position and model parameters, and little on update scheme or time step~\cite{xu2021}. Social force simulations form transient blocking clusters, some attached to the walls~\cite{parisi2005microscopic,cornes2021microscopic}.

\subsubsection{Activated Escape.}
A particle kicked by thermal noise leaves a well at rate $\lambda=\nu\exp(-\Delta U/k_{\rm B}T_{\rm b})$, with barrier $\Delta U$, bath temperature $T_{\rm b}$ and attempt frequency $\nu$~\cite{arrhenius1889,kramers1940}; in many dimensions the lowest saddle sets the rate~\cite{hanggi1990}, with Langer's prefactor~\cite{langer1969}. The string method finds it by relaxing a discretised path until it is parallel to the force~\cite{e2002,e2007}. The Br{\"u}nger--Brooks--Karplus Langevin scheme misses the bath temperature by $O(\Delta t)$, and the Gr{\o}nbech-Jensen--Farago scheme does not~\cite{gronbechjensen2013}. For self-propelled noise the escape rate depends on the whole barrier shape~\cite{woillez2019}.

\subsubsection{Deadlock in Multi-Agent Systems.}
Multi-robot systems avoiding collisions through control barrier functions deadlock at force-balance equilibria reached from symmetric configurations, which a provably correct switching controller resolves~\cite{grover2023,grover2020} and broken symmetry helps avoid~\cite{grover2020symmetry}. ORCA deadlocks in symmetric robot scenes~\cite{liu2026orcadeadlock} and fails in tight passages, where local multi-agent path finding assists it~\cite{dergachev2021case}. The pair standoff (Sect.~\ref{sec:pair}) is such a force-balance equilibrium between two pedestrians.

\subsubsection{Testing Mechanisms in Models.}
Pattern-oriented modelling asks a model to reproduce several patterns because one pattern rarely discriminates between mechanisms~\cite{grimm2005}. Here the rival mechanism is a surrogate matched to the credited one in freed fraction (Sect.~\ref{sec:protocol}), and the patterns are probe responses.
