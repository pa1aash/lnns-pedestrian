\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}
The deadlocks of this social force model at a narrow door are rest states set by the wall range and the door-edge segment ends; with the default walls half the runs complete at $w=\RevBtwoWfifty$\,m. Force-coupled ORCA frees the lone trap through its rest drive, \RevAoneDriveRatioLo--\RevAoneDriveRatioHi\ times that of SFM, and the SFM drive with relaxation time $\tau'=\RevAoneTauPrimeLo$--$\RevAoneTauPrimeHi$\,s and that rest drive frees the same lone keystones; replacing the drive by the ORCA force frees only the lone keystone the stuck-alone criterion predicts. In whole runs its drive part alone completes \RwOneDriveK\ of \RwOneN\ runs against its own \RwOneOrcaK, so the constraint part lowers completion. Noise matched to it at horizon $T$ frees deadlocks by fluctuations, and a matched lateral bias tilts the pair standoff out of its minimum. Activated escape is established only with the blocker alone at $\beta\ge1.5$, with an Arrhenius slope \RzTtwoRatio\ of the computed barrier; the shortfall from 1 comes from the prefactor of the anharmonic well, not from friction. With uncalibrated defaults the variants pass \RevDThreeRatioMin--\RevDThreeRatioMax\ of the human flow over whole CroMa runs, and \RvBoneCroOffMin--\RvBoneCroOffMax\ without the density throttle. A narrow-door deadlock count should not be read as crowd behaviour until probes name its mechanism and matched experiments test its flow and passage gaps.
