\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}
The deadlocks of this social force model at a narrow door are rest states set by the wall range and the door-edge segment ends; with the default walls half the runs complete at $w=\RevBtwoWfifty$\,m. Force-coupled ORCA frees the lone trap through its rest drive, as the same model with $\tau'=\RevAoneTauPrimeLo$--$\RevAoneTauPrimeHi$\,s and \RevAoneDriveRatioLo--\RevAoneDriveRatioHi\ times the rest drive does. In whole runs its drive part alone completes \RwOneDriveK\ of \RwOneN\ runs against its own \RwOneOrcaK, so the constraint part lowers completion. Noise matched to it at horizon $T$ frees deadlocks by fluctuations, and a matched lateral bias tilts the pair standoff out of its minimum. Activated escape, with an Arrhenius slope \RvAthreeRatioDeep--\RvAthreeRatioMid\ of the computed barrier, is established only with the blocker alone at $\beta\ge1.5$; its attempt frequency, a median \FixAthreeNuRatioAll\ of the damped Langer prediction, remains unexplained. With uncalibrated defaults every variant passes at most \RevDThreeRatioMin--\RevDThreeRatioMax\ of the human flow over whole CroMa runs, and \RvBoneCroOffMin--\RvBoneCroOffMax\ without the density throttle. A narrow-door deadlock count should not be read as crowd behaviour until probes name its mechanism and matched experiments test its flow and passage gaps.
