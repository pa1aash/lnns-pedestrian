\paragraph{Steering paradigms.}
The SFM writes the motion of each pedestrian as Newton's equation with a relaxation towards a desired velocity and exponential repulsion from other pedestrians and walls~\cite{helbing1995}; the extension used for crowd crushes adds a body-compression force and a sliding friction that act only in contact~\cite{helbing2000nature}. The TTC law of Karamouzas et al.\ was inferred from trajectory statistics: the interaction energy depends on the predicted time to collision $\tau_{ij}$ and falls off as $\tau_{ij}^{-2}$ with an exponential cut-off~\cite{karamouzas2014}. Velocity-obstacle methods take a geometric route. The velocity obstacle of a neighbour is the set of own velocities that lead to contact within a time horizon~\cite{fiorini1998}; ORCA shares the avoidance between the two agents and reduces each obstacle to a half-plane so that the new velocity is the solution of a two-dimensional linear program nearest to the preferred velocity~\cite{vandenberg2011}. The reference implementation is the RVO2 library~\cite{vandenberg2011rvo2}. Our model combines all three as force terms, so that each can be switched off at a frozen state.

\paragraph{Clogging at bottlenecks.}
Grains in a silo, colloids, sheep and people passing a narrow door all alternate between bursts of flow and clogs. Zuriguel et al.\ proposed that the flow is clogged when the mean duration of the stoppages diverges, which happens when their distribution has a heavy enough tail~\cite{zuriguel2014}. In granular hoppers the clog is an arch held by contact forces and friction; vibration breaks it. Nicolas, Garcimart\'in and Zuriguel described unclogging under vibration as escape from traps whose depths are spread over a distribution, which accounts for broad clog-time statistics without invoking a single barrier~\cite{nicolas2018trap}. In pedestrian models, boundary parameters alone decide whether a velocity-based model clogs~\cite{xu2021}, and the SFM forms transient blocking clusters~\cite{parisi2005microscopic}, some attached to the walls~\cite{cornes2021microscopic}. The SFM considered here has no static friction, so it cannot hold a frictional arch. Its deadlocks are rest states of a driven, damped system, and we ask what each intervention does to those rest states.

\paragraph{Activated escape.}
A particle held in a potential well and kicked by thermal noise leaves the well at a rate $\lambda=\nu\exp(-\Delta U/k_BT)$, where $\Delta U$ is the barrier height and $\nu$ an attempt rate~\cite{arrhenius1889,kramers1940}; H\"anggi, Talkner and Borkovec reviewed the theory, including the many-dimensional case in which the rate is set by the lowest saddle between two minima~\cite{hanggi1990}. In many dimensions the barrier is found along a minimum-energy path. The string method evolves a discretised path until it is everywhere parallel to the force~\cite{e2002,e2007}. For noise injected into a damped velocity recursion, the stationary variance of the recursion fixes an effective temperature that depends on the time step~\cite{gronbechjensen2013}; we use this dependence as a signature (Sect.~\ref{sec:teff}). The equilibrium form applies because our rest field is a gradient and the noise is additive; for self-propelled noise the rate depends on the whole shape of the barrier~\cite{woillez2020}.

\paragraph{Deadlock in multi-agent systems.}
Navigation along a potential gradient has long been known to stall in local minima of the navigation potential~\cite{koren1991}. In multi-robot systems that avoid collisions through control barrier functions, Grover et al.\ characterised deadlocks as equilibria of the closed-loop dynamics, analysed their stability and proposed perturbation strategies to leave them~\cite{grover2020}. The safety constraints of those robots play the role that ORCA's half-planes play here. Symmetric configurations deadlock such robots and added asymmetry helps them escape~\cite{grover2020symmetry}, and ORCA itself deadlocks in symmetric robot scenes~\cite{liu2026orcadeadlock}. The pair standoff of Sect.~\ref{sec:pair} is the pedestrian analogue. The controls we test, noise and a lateral bias, are perturbations of this kind.

\paragraph{Testing mechanisms in models.}
Pattern-oriented modelling asks that a model reproduce several patterns observed at different scales, because a single pattern rarely discriminates between candidate mechanisms~\cite{grimm2005}. Our protocol shares the premise but differs in two ways. The rival explanation is a control fitted to the paradigm's own headline score, and the discriminating patterns are interventional responses of a frozen deadlock, predicted from the physics of each mechanism class. We have not found an earlier study testing a matched control this way.
