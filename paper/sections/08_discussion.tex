\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Mechanisms Behind a Matched Rate}
Three mechanisms reach the same rate: ORCA's rest drive removes the fixed point (class L), noise lifts the blocker over its barrier at a rate set by $\Delta U/k_BT_{\rm eff}$ (class T), and the lateral bias tilts the pair minimum until it is lost (class D). A matched rate is therefore weak evidence of a shared mechanism. The probes separate each surrogate from ORCA, and the rest-drive push, of ORCA's class, agrees with it on every probe at the lone keystone.

\subsection{Relation to Clogging and Robot Deadlock}
Granular and pedestrian clogging is read through arches and clog-lifetime statistics~\cite{zuriguel2014,nicolas2018trap}. Both deadlock types are set by the wall geometry at the door edges: the lone trap by the jamb's wall range, the loss of the pair minimum at $b_f$ by the end of the jamb segment. At the lone trap noise acts by the activated escape of trap models, over the model's own barriers, with only the attempt rate fitted. Multi-robot deadlocks under safety constraints are closed-loop equilibria left by designed perturbations~\cite{grover2020}, at which a resolver and its rate-matched surrogate can be probed together.

\subsection{Using the Framework}\label{sec:framework}
The open framework deadlock-screen implements the screening protocol (\url{https://github.com/pa1aash/deadlock-screen}). It needs from a steering model a restartable state, deterministic replay, a rest field conservative at frozen densities and switches for single force terms. Quench, replay and the corrected implementation are generic probes. The time-step probe needs a noise term, the push and tilt probes a goal-directed drive. Before reading a narrow-door deadlock rate as crowd behaviour, a modeller should:
\begin{enumerate}
\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{(\roman{enumi})}
\item compare $g_{\max}(r)$ with $m v_0/\tau$ over the population (Eq.~\eqref{eq:stuck});
\item use the nearest point of the jamb--corridor wall union at a door edge;
\item report ORCA results per solver and coupling: the legacy solver changes outcomes under $10^{-9}$\,m replays (Sect.~\ref{sec:att-lone}), and velocity coupling frees no deadlock (Sect.~\ref{sec:velorca});
\item treat per-step noise, used for unjamming since~\cite{helbing2000freezing}, as having a $\Delta t$-dependent temperature (Eq.~\eqref{eq:teff});
\item test against the completion, flow and gaps of matched experiments (Sect.~\ref{sec:validity}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations}
Agents are two-dimensional discs, 100 per room run, in one family of door geometries near $w=0.8$\,m, with human data at that width only. The deadlocks are those of the last agents at a narrow door at the end of an evacuation; the rest-state analysis, at frozen densities, describes them once formed. The pair library was built under a short-range wall law ($B_w=0.04$\,m on every wall), and velocity-coupled ORCA was screened by quench, replay and whole runs only. Whole runs end at 600\,s, and the \PairN\ pair standoffs give wide 95\% intervals for rate differences (Table~\ref{tab:matched}).
