\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Matched Outcomes}
In this social force model a freed fraction matched at horizon $T$ is weak evidence of a shared mechanism: force-coupled ORCA, the matched noise and the lateral bias free deadlocks through different forces (Tables~\ref{tab:signatures} and~\ref{tab:observed}), while the rest-drive push agrees with force-coupled ORCA on every defining probe.

\subsection{Limitations}
Agents are two-dimensional discs, 100 per room run, and the deadlocks are those of the last agents of an evacuation. The lone trap originates in the default wall range ($B_w=0.08$\,m), the pair standoff in sharp segment ends with a short wall range (pair set built with $B_w=0.04$\,m). Force coupling is not ORCA's published form, and native ORCA is untested (Sect.~\ref{sec:identity}). The density throttle on the desired speed (Eq.~\eqref{eq:des}) is not part of the original social force model~\cite{helbing1995,helbing2000nature} and shapes the rest drive.

The stuck-alone fraction depends on the radius distribution through $g_{\max}(r)$.

Because $k_{\rm B}T_{\rm eff}\propto m_i\tau_i$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:teff}) and $\tau_i$ spreads over 0.4--0.6\,s, agents differ in temperature, and the single $\beta$ of a snapshot uses the blocker's $\tau$.

All runs use $\Delta t=0.01$\,s; only the arches at \ArchVZero\,m/s were checked at finer steps.

A fraction match holds within a window of horizons (Sect.~\ref{sec:protocol}), and the \PairN\ pair snapshots give 95\% intervals as wide as [$\TabThreePairPubDiffLo$, $\TabThreePairPubDiffHi$] for differences in freed fraction (Table~\ref{tab:matched-bias}). The matched noise levels lie below the tested range $\beta\ge1.5$ (Sects.~\ref{sec:escape} and~\ref{sec:att-whole}), so the whole-run stall prediction extrapolates the Arrhenius law outside the Kramers regime. All results come from door geometries near $w=0.8$\,m, the width of the human data.
