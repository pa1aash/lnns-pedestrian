\paragraph{Calibration and test.}
A matched control tests whether a simpler cause suffices for the paradigm's score; in all three pairs it does: noise at $\sigma=0.07$\,m/s frees lone keystones at ORCA's rate ($\Delta=\TabThreeLKDiff$), noise at $\sigma^\ast$ completes whole runs at its rate ($\Delta=\EsixDiff$ [$\EsixDiffLo$, $\EsixDiffHi$]), and a bias at $b^\ast$ frees pair standoffs at the rate of either solver. On the same deadlocks the signatures of Sect.~\ref{sec:signatures} separate each control from ORCA: replay and time to free at the lone trap, time to completion and final-state class in whole runs, handedness and identity at the pair. Identity separates only at the pair, where both resolvers are deterministic and each overlaps itself completely; at the lone trap the unresolved sets of ORCA and noise overlap about as much as two halves of the noise seeds ($J=\FixAoneLoneJ$ against \FixAoneJNoiseNoise), and in whole runs the failure sets more than independent ones (\EsixJaccard\ against \EsixJaccardIndep). Controls from other classes differ from ORCA on their own class's probes by construction; the rest-drive push, a constant force of class L, could have gone either way and agrees with ORCA on every probe at the lone keystone.

\paragraph{One rate, different mechanisms.}
At rest ORCA's velocity, added as a force, is a second goal drive that pushes the stuck-alone margin below zero and removes the fixed point (class L). Noise keeps the fixed point and crosses its barrier at a rate set by $\Delta U/k_BT_{\rm eff}$ (class T). The bias tilts the pair's strict minimum until it is lost (class D). Summed over the deadlock population, different mechanisms reach the same completion rate.

\paragraph{Checklist for modellers.}
(i)~Before reading a deadlock rate at a narrow door as crowd behaviour, compare $g_{\max}(r)$ with $m v_0/\tau$ over the population (Eq.~\eqref{eq:stuck}); crowding decides only whether others push a stuck-alone agent through. (ii)~Where a jamb and a corridor wall meet at a door edge, take the nearest point on the union of the walls. (iii)~Report ORCA results per solver and coupling: the published linear program changes outcome in \EtwoReplayCThreePubFlips\ of \EtwoReplayN\ lone-trap replays, and only ORCA added as a force has a rest drive. (iv)~The temperature of per-step noise, an unjamming device since~\cite{helbing2000freezing}, depends on the time step (Eq.~\eqref{eq:teff}), so a level tuned at one $\Delta t$ is different physics at another. (v)~Check a narrow-door model against completion, flow and gap statistics from matched experiments, since removing deadlocks does not restore capacity (Sect.~\ref{sec:validity}).

\paragraph{Applying the protocol.}
The protocol needs a restartable state, deterministic replay, a rest field conservative at frozen densities, and switches for single force terms. Quench, replay and corrected numerics are generic; the time-step probe needs a noise term, and the push and tilt probes a goal-directed drive.

\paragraph{Clogging and robot deadlock.}
Granular and pedestrian clogging is read through arches and clog-lifetime statistics~\cite{zuriguel2014,nicolas2018trap}. Our deadlocks are soft-core analogues of arches, held by repulsion of range $B_w$ rather than by contact and friction. At the lone trap noise frees them by the activated escape that trap models assume, over barriers from the model's own landscape, fitting only the attempt rate. Multi-robot deadlocks under safety constraints are closed-loop equilibria, left by designed perturbations~\cite{grover2020}. The protocol carries over to any resolver credited with removing an equilibrium: match a control to its success rate and probe both on the frozen equilibria.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Agents are two-dimensional discs, 100 per run, in one family of door geometries near $w=0.8$\,m, with human data at that width only. The deadlocks are those of the last agents at a narrow door, at the end of an evacuation. The pair standoff needs a short-range jamb law. ORCA enters as an added force; velocity-level ORCA is outside our scope. Whole runs end at 600\,s. The \PairN\ pair standoffs give wide 95\% intervals for rate differences (Table~\ref{tab:matched}). The rest-state analysis, at frozen densities, describes deadlocks once formed.
