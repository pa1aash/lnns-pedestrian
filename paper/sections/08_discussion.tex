\paragraph{What testing adds to calibration.}
A matched control shows whether a simpler cause suffices for the paradigm's score, and in all three of our pairs it does. Noise at $\sigma=0.07$\,m/s frees lone keystones at ORCA's rate ($\Delta=\TabThreeLKDiff$), noise at $\sigma^\ast$ completes whole runs at its rate ($\Delta=\EsixDiff$ [\EsixDiffLo, \EsixDiffHi]), and a bias at $b^\ast$ frees pair standoffs at the rate of either solver. Read alone, as in the conference protocol~\cite{gang2026}, each match suggests that ORCA does what its control does. The held-out signatures, derived from the physics before the runs, reject that reading each time. The minority outcome sets overlap little ($J=\EtwoLKJaccardC$ at the lone trap; \EsixJaccard\ in whole runs, against \EsixJaccardIndep\ for independent sets; \PairBsPubJac\ and \TabThreePairRvoJacF\ at the pair), and each control fails a probe of its own class: the time step and seed replay for noise, handedness for the bias.

\paragraph{One rate, different mechanisms.}
The signatures also say why the rates coincide. At rest ORCA's velocity is a second goal drive that pushes the stuck-alone margin below zero, removing the fixed point (class L). Noise keeps the fixed point and crosses its barrier at a rate set by $\Delta U/k_BT_{\rm eff}$ (class T). The bias tilts the pair's strict minimum until it vanishes at a fold (class D). A completion rate sums whichever of these acts over the deadlock population, and different mechanisms reach the same sum from different deadlocks, as the low overlaps show. The rate is a calibration target, not a test of mechanism.

\paragraph{Implications for model users.}
First, the lone trap is a property of the jamb wall law, not of crowding (Eq.~\eqref{eq:stuck}; Sect.~\ref{sec:validity}): a deadlock rate at a narrow door should be read against the stuck-alone margin before it is read as crowd behaviour. Second, ORCA results are statements about a solver. The published linear program and RVO2 free \PairPubFreed\ and \PairRvoFreed\ of \PairN\ pair standoffs, and only the published program changes outcome under $10^{-9}$\,m replays (\EtwoReplayCThreePubFlips\ of \EtwoReplayN\ lone-trap snapshots); results should name a validated solver. Third, noise is not a neutral way to unstick a model: its temperature depends on the time step (Eq.~\eqref{eq:teff}), so a level tuned at one $\Delta t$ is different physics at another.

\paragraph{Clogging and robot deadlock.}
Clogging of grains and pedestrians is read through arches and clog-lifetime statistics~\cite{zuriguel2014,nicolas2018trap}. Our deadlocks are contact-free rest states of a frictionless driven model, yet noise frees them by the activated escape that trap models assume, over barriers computed from the model rather than fitted to it. In multi-robot navigation, deadlocks under safety constraints are equilibria of the closed loop, left by designed perturbations~\cite{grover2020}. Wherever a resolver is credited with removing an equilibrium, the protocol carries over: match a control to its success rate and probe both on the frozen equilibria.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Agents are discs in two dimensions; we studied one family of door geometries, room exits near $w=0.8$\,m, and compared with human data at that width only. Samples are finite: \PairN\ pair standoffs cannot show rate equivalence within $\pm0.10$. The rest-state analysis freezes the densities, so it describes a deadlock once formed, not its formation.
