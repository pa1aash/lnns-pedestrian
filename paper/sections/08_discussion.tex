\paragraph{Calibration and test.}
In all three settings a matched control reaches the paradigm's rate: noise at the lone trap and in whole runs, a bias at $b^\ast$ for either solver at the pair standoff (Sect.~\ref{sec:attribution}, Table~\ref{tab:matched}). The bias match holds on its calibration snapshots by construction. On the same deadlocks the signatures of Sect.~\ref{sec:signatures} separate each control from ORCA: replay and time to free at the lone trap, time to completion and final-state class in whole runs, handedness and identity at the pair. Identity separates only there, where both resolvers are deterministic. The rest-drive push agrees with ORCA on every probe at the lone keystone. Across the deadlocks different mechanisms reach the same rate: ORCA's rest drive removes the fixed point (class L); noise crosses its barrier at a rate set by $\Delta U/k_BT_{\rm eff}$ (class T); the bias tilts the pair's minimum until it is lost (class D).

\paragraph{Checklist for modellers.}
(i)~Before reading a narrow-door deadlock rate as crowd behaviour, compare $g_{\max}(r)$ with $m v_0/\tau$ over the population (Eq.~\eqref{eq:stuck}). (ii)~At a door edge take the nearest point on the union of jamb and corridor wall. (iii)~Report ORCA results per solver and coupling: the published linear program changes lone-trap outcomes under $10^{-9}$\,m replays (Sect.~\ref{sec:att-lone}); only ORCA added as a force has a rest drive. (iv)~Per-step noise, an unjamming device since~\cite{helbing2000freezing}, has a temperature that depends on $\Delta t$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:teff}). (v)~Test against completion, flow and gap statistics of matched experiments; removing deadlocks does not restore capacity (Sect.~\ref{sec:validity}).

\paragraph{Applying the protocol.}
The protocol needs a restartable state, deterministic replay, a rest field conservative at frozen densities and switches for single force terms. Quench, replay and corrected numerics are generic; the time-step probe needs a noise term, the push and tilt probes a goal-directed drive.

\paragraph{Clogging and robot deadlock.}
Granular and pedestrian clogging is read through arches and clog-lifetime statistics~\cite{zuriguel2014,nicolas2018trap}. Our deadlocks are soft-core analogues of arches, held by repulsion of range $B_w$ rather than by contact and friction. At the lone trap noise frees them by the activated escape of trap models, over the model's own barriers, fitting only the attempt rate. Multi-robot deadlocks under safety constraints are closed-loop equilibria left by designed perturbations~\cite{grover2020}; the protocol applies there: match a control to the resolver's success rate and probe both at the equilibria.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Agents are two-dimensional discs, 100 per run, in one family of door geometries near $w=0.8$\,m, with human data at that width only. The deadlocks are those of the last agents at a narrow door, at the end of an evacuation; the rest-state analysis, at frozen densities, describes them once formed. The pair standoff needs a short-range jamb law. ORCA enters as an added force; velocity-level ORCA is outside our scope. Whole runs end at 600\,s, and the \PairN\ pair standoffs give wide 95\% intervals for rate differences (Table~\ref{tab:matched}).
