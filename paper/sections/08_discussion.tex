\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Mechanisms Behind a Matched Freed Fraction}
In this social force model three mechanisms reach the same freed fraction at a stated horizon $T$: force-coupled ORCA's rest drive removes the rest state (Reshape, axial subtype), noise carries the blocker over its barrier (Heat), and the lateral bias tilts the pair minimum until it is lost (Reshape, lateral subtype). A matched freed fraction is therefore weak evidence of a shared mechanism. The probes separate each surrogate from force-coupled ORCA, and the rest-drive push, of the same class, agrees with it on every defining probe (Table~\ref{tab:signatures}).

\subsection{Relation to Clogging and Robot Deadlock}
Granular and pedestrian clogging is read through arches and clog-lifetime statistics~\cite{zuriguel2014,nicolas2018trap}; here noise frees the lone trap by activated escape over the model's own barriers, with the attempt rate fitted as in trap models. Multi-robot deadlocks are also closed-loop equilibria, which Grover et al.\ resolve with a switching controller because additive perturbations carry no guarantee~\cite{grover2023}.

\subsection{Limitations}
Agents are two-dimensional discs, 100 per room run, and the deadlocks are those of the last agents at the end of an evacuation. The lone trap originates in the default wall range ($B_w=0.08$\,m) and the pair standoff in sharp segment ends: the pair library was built with $B_w=0.04$\,m on every wall, and rounded door edges remove the standoff. Force-coupled ORCA is a non-standard coupling; native ORCA, which sets velocities and treats walls as obstacles, is untested. A fraction match holds within a window of horizons (from \RevAeightLKNoiseCMatchFrom\,s at the lone keystone, from \RevAeightWholeNoiseMatchFrom\,s in whole runs), so each match is stated at its $T$, and the \PairN\ pair snapshots give wide 95\% intervals for differences in freed fraction (Table~\ref{tab:matched-bias}). All results come from one family of door geometries near $w=0.8$\,m, the width of the human data.
