\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
This section asks how different mechanisms reach one rate and what a model needs for the screening protocol to apply.

\subsection{Mechanisms Behind a Matched Rate}
Three mechanisms reach the same rate: ORCA's rest drive removes the fixed point (class L), noise carries the blocker over its barrier at a rate set by $\Delta U/k_BT_{\rm eff}$ (class T), and the lateral bias tilts the pair minimum until it is lost (class D). A matched rate is therefore weak evidence of a shared mechanism; the probes separate each surrogate from ORCA, while the rest-drive push, of ORCA's class, agrees with it on every probe at the lone keystone.

\subsection{Deadlocks, Clogging and Robot Deadlock}
Granular and pedestrian clogging is read through arches and clog-lifetime statistics~\cite{zuriguel2014,nicolas2018trap}. Both deadlock types are set by the wall geometry at the door edges: the lone trap by the jamb's wall range, the loss of the pair minimum at $b_f$ by the end of the jamb segment. At the lone trap noise frees the blocker by the activated escape of trap models, over the model's own barriers; only the attempt rate is fitted. Multi-robot deadlocks under safety constraints are closed-loop equilibria left by designed perturbations~\cite{grover2020}; there a surrogate matched to the resolver's success rate can be probed with it at the equilibria.

\subsection{Using the Framework}\label{sec:framework}
The open framework deadlock-screen (\url{https://github.com/pa1aash/deadlock-screen}; see Data and Code Availability) implements the screening protocol. It needs from a steering model a restartable state, deterministic replay, a rest field conservative at frozen densities and switches for single force terms. Quench, replay and the corrected implementation are generic probes. The time-step probe needs a noise term, the push and tilt probes a goal-directed drive. Before reading a narrow-door deadlock rate as crowd behaviour, a modeller should:
\begin{enumerate}
\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{(\roman{enumi})}
\item compare $g_{\max}(r)$ with $m v_0/\tau$ over the population (Eq.~\eqref{eq:stuck});
\item at a door edge, take the nearest point on the union of jamb and corridor wall;
\item report ORCA results per solver and per coupling, because the legacy solver changes outcomes under $10^{-9}$\,m replays (Sect.~\ref{sec:att-lone}) and only force coupling has a rest drive (Sect.~\ref{sec:velorca});
\item treat per-step noise, used for unjamming since~\cite{helbing2000freezing}, as having a $\Delta t$-dependent temperature (Eq.~\eqref{eq:teff});
\item test against the completion, flow and gaps of matched experiments (Sect.~\ref{sec:validity}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations}
Agents are two-dimensional discs, 100 per room run, in one family of door geometries near $w=0.8$\,m, with human data at that width only. The deadlocks are those of the last agents at a narrow door at the end of an evacuation; the rest-state analysis, at frozen densities, describes them once formed. The pair standoff needs a short-range jamb law. ORCA is screened under force coupling; velocity coupling is treated in Sect.~\ref{sec:velorca}. Whole runs end at 600\,s, and the \PairN\ pair standoffs give wide 95\% intervals for rate differences (Table~\ref{tab:matched}).
