# Alt text for the figures

One or two sentences per figure, for the proof stage. Each describes what is drawn. Figures are listed in the order of
the revised paper (geometry figure in Sect. 3, the time-step figure merged into the escape figure); the source file is
given in brackets. Script numbers (fig2 to fig8) are historical and do not follow the paper's figure numbers.

**Fig. 1 (fig1_hero.tex: fig1_hero.png with fig1_overlay.tex).** Two rendered scenes at a 0.8 m door: above, a light-grey blocker held alone between two darker neighbours that pass alone, with force arrows for its own drive, the neighbours' push, force-coupled ORCA's rest drive and the opposing wall force; below, two navy members held together against the jamb faces, with drive, wall and partner arrows. Beside each scene, energy curves with axes in metres and joules, each with a black marker at the rest state, show a 21 J barrier above a 10 J vermilion noise band for the blocker, which the added drive turns into a downhill slope, and a 0.4 J dip for the pair, which the lateral tilt removes.

**Fig. 2 (fig_geometry.pdf).** Two to-scale floor plans with direct labels. Left: a 10 m square room with a dashed spawn rectangle, a 0.8 m door in its right wall and a goal disc outside, and an enlarged inset of the door showing the door edges, the shaded jamb stretches where the edge wall range applies and where it blends out, one agent of radius 0.25 m, and the goal with its capture disc; right: a 7.8 by 8.6 m holding area, a grey 2.2 by 4.0 m waiting zone in front of the 0.8 m entrance, a 2.0 m corridor with a dotted line at 0.2 m for the short bottleneck, a goal disc, and dashed outlines of the thicker, tapered walls of the experiment beside the model's thin walls.

**Fig. 3 (fig_protocol.tex).** A greyscale flowchart of five boxes in a row joined by arrows: snapshots (lone keystones, second traps, pair standoffs); probes (quench, replay, time step with per-step or √Δt-scaled noise, rest-drive push ±, lateral tilt ±b, RVO2 solver); signatures (freed fraction at T, times to free, Δt dependence, handedness under tilt, replay sensitivity); class (Prevent; Reshape, axial or lateral; Heat), with a separate dashed box below it for the defect flag (outcome vanishes with the RVO2 solver); and the comparison of the resolver with its fraction-matched surrogate.

**Fig. 4 (fig2_lone_trap.pdf).** Left: the backward wall force on a lone agent on the door axis rises to a peak just before the door plane, drawn for four agent radii, with a grey band and black median line for the SFM rest drives (about 215 N) and a blue hatched band and blue median line for the total rest drives under force-coupled ORCA (RVO2, about 430 N). Right: a cloud of 2000 spawned agents over agent radius and v0/τ, with the black SFM margin-zero line (stuck side shaded grey) and two blue force-coupled ORCA lines further right.

**Fig. 5 (fig3_width_walls.pdf).** Left: the fraction of SFM runs completed, at eleven door widths from 0.7 to 1.0 m with 100 runs each, rises along an S-shaped fitted curve with a thin grey band from 0 to 1 and passes one half at 0.88 m, inside a light grey vertical span from 0.75 to 0.92 m that marks the bracket on that width. Right: one horizontal bar per wall-law variant at 0.8 m, split into completed (white), pair or group standoff (light grey) and lone trap (dark grey), with the interval of the completed fraction drawn as a whisker at the white segment's end; only the short-range wall laws complete most runs.

**Fig. 6 (fig_escape_dt.pdf).** (a) The log escape rate divided by the fitted attempt rate falls along a line of slope −1 against β, the barrier over the effective thermal energy, out to β = 7, where the black high-barrier restarts lie slightly above the line. (b) The fitted Arrhenius slope over the barrier lies mostly between 0.5 and 1.5 for barriers from 1 to 100 J; three black fits over β up to 7 sit near 0.9, close to a dashed reference line. (c) On log–log axes the escape rate under per-step noise, relative to Δt = 0.01 s, falls as the time step grows, along its prediction curves, while √Δt-scaled noise and the inverse time to free under force-coupled ORCA (RVO2) stay at one.

**Fig. 7 (fig6_quench.pdf).** Left: a dot plot of seven perturbations in two groups (force-coupled ORCA and its parts; noise) giving the fraction of lone-trap blockers freed within 120 s with intervals, filled dots for lone keystones and open dots, set slightly lower, for second traps. Right: step curves with bands of the fraction of lone keystones freed against log time; force-coupled ORCA (RVO2) rises within a few seconds, noise σ = 0.07 m/s over tens of seconds, and both reach about 0.9 by 120 s.

**Fig. 8 (fig7_pair.pdf).** Left: the fraction of pairs freed is zero at zero bias and climbs on both sides to one, following a grey static-prediction staircase, with horizontal lines at the legacy (0.46) and RVO2 (0.15) force-coupled ORCA fractions. Right, after a column of row labels: a dot plot of pairs freed per perturbation in two groups (force-coupled ORCA and its parts; bias ±b* per solver).

**Fig. 9 (fig8_croma.pdf).** Three panels share one list of five categories (humans, SFM, SFM with the jamb wall range, force-coupled ORCA (RVO2), SFM with noise σ*): flow, longest gap between passages on a log axis with the 600 s horizon as a vertical line, and waiting-zone velocity fluctuation. Each run is a small marker, a square for the 2.0 m and a circle for the 0.2 m bottleneck, open if the run did not complete, and each category's mean is a vertical bar on its interval; the open markers in the gap panel are lower bounds, most near 400–470 s, while every human run flows faster than every model run, with longest gaps of 1.4–2.0 s.
