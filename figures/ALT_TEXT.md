# Alt text for Figs 1–8

One or two sentences per figure, for the proof stage. Each describes what is drawn.

**Fig. 1.** Two rendered scenes at a 0.8 m door: above, a lone agent trapped at the door edges with force arrows for its drive, the neighbours' push, ORCA's rest drive and the opposing wall force; below, two agents held side by side against the jamb faces in a pair standoff. Beside each scene a small energy curve shows the rest-state minimum before and after the rest-drive push or the lateral tilt removes it, with a vermilion band marking the effective thermal energy.

**Fig. 2.** Left: the backward wall force on a lone agent on the door axis rises steeply towards the door plane, drawn for four agent radii, with horizontal bands showing the range of goal drives for C1 (black) and for C3 with ORCA (blue). Right: a colour map of the stuck-alone margin over agent radius and v0/τ, overlaid with the 20 000 spawned agents as grey dots and with the C1 and C3 boundaries between agents that pass alone and agents that are held.

**Fig. 3.** Left: the fraction of C1 runs completed falls as the exit width narrows from 1.0 m to 0.7 m. Right: at 0.8 m, one point with its interval per wall-law variant, each beside a stacked bar splitting the runs into completed (white), pair or group standoff (light grey) and lone trap (dark grey).

**Fig. 4.** Left: three step curves of the fraction of blockers escaped against time under noise, for the full, pinned and alone barrier conditions. Right: the logarithm of the escape rate over the attempt rate falls along a straight line against the barrier height over the thermal energy, with points for every snapshot, condition and noise level.

**Fig. 5.** Escape rate against time step on log–log axes, normalised at Δt = 0.01 s: per-step noise (vermilion, filled markers) falls with the time step along its Arrhenius prediction lines, while √Δt-scaled noise (light vermilion, open markers) and C3 with ORCA (blue diamonds) stay close to one.

**Fig. 6.** Left: the fraction of lone-trap blockers freed within 120 s for each intervention, ORCA part, rest-drive push, lateral bias and noise level, with filled points for lone keystones and open points for second traps. Right: step curves of the fraction freed against time on a log axis for the interventions that free blockers, with C3 in the published-reproduction mode dash-dotted.

**Fig. 7.** Left: the fraction of pair standoffs freed is lowest at zero bias and rises steeply on either side to one as the signed lateral bias grows, with yellow measured points, a dark grey static prediction and horizontal lines at the two C3 rates. Right: the fraction of pairs freed within 120 s for each intervention, ORCA part, rest-drive push, noise level and matched bias of either sign.

**Fig. 8.** Three panels compare the five human runs in the CroMa bottleneck with each model variant in the matched geometry: flow through the entrance, the longest gap between successive passages on a log axis, and the waiting-zone velocity fluctuation. Single runs are small points and each variant's mean is a larger marker with its interval; every model variant flows well below the humans.
