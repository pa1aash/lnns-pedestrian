# A1 Radius sensitivity of the stuck-alone fraction

Script `experiments/revision2/A1_radius.py` -> `data/derived/rev2_A1.json`; registry `notes/C2_items/A1.yaml`
(prefix RvAone). CPU time 5 s. No simulations.

## Data, rule, method
- Population: the 20 000 spawned agents of E1b (seeds 42-241, 100 agents each) that give the paper's
  PphysStuckAloneFrac = 0.23 and StuckAloneFracCThree = 0.013 (`E1_analysis.json` E1b.stuck_alone_frac_C1 /
  _C3_rest, from the per-agent `margin_C1`, `margin_C3` of `data/E1/E1_runs.jsonl`).
- Attributes are regenerated with the scenario's own sampler `AgentState.create(seed)` (code/sim/core/agent.py:
  r = max(N(mu_r, 0.03), 0.15) m, v0 = max(N(1.34, 0.26), 0.5) m/s, tau ~ U(0.4, 0.6) s, m = 80 kg, one PCG64(seed)
  stream). At mu_r = 0.25 m the regenerated attributes equal the E1b record exactly (maximum difference 0). At
  mu_r = 0.225 m the same stream gives the same v0 and tau and every raw radius draw shifted by -0.025 m before the
  clip at 0.15 m (6 clipped agents at 0.25 m, 107 at 0.225 m).
- Stuck alone: g_max(r) > F_rest, g_max(r) = `w2lib.Passability(0.8).gmax` (largest backward wall/jamb force on a
  lone agent at rest on the door axis, code's WallForces, A_w = 2000 N, B_w = 0.08 m, sharp jambs; the grid value at
  0.225 m equals `w2lib.max_backward` to 4e-14 N).
  (i) SFM: F_rest = m v0/tau (`w2lib.drive`, kind C1, rho = 0; Weidmann factor 1).
  (ii) force-coupled ORCA: F_rest = m v0/tau + w_o(0) m v0/tau_orca (kind C3; w_o(0) = 0.99966, tau_orca = 0.5 s),
  the code's rest force, equal to the SFM with tau' = tau tau_orca/(tau + tau_orca) and target speed
  (v0/tau + w_o v0/tau_orca) tau' (rev A1 identity). Both are the rules that produced `margin_C1` / `margin_C3`.
- Wilson 95% intervals. The change is paired by agent: every agent stuck alone at 0.225 m is stuck alone at 0.25 m
  (g_max rises with r, the drive is unchanged), so no test is needed.

**Checked (item A9):** the data paper (Boomers et al., Collective Dynamics 8 (2023) A141, p. 7) reports a mean
shoulder width of 0.45 m (SD 0.04 m) over all 1038 participants (\RvAnineShoulderWidth, \RvAnineShoulderWidthSD), so
0.225 m is half the participants' mean shoulder width.

## Interpretation
The door-edge force is a sum of exponentials in (r - d)/B_w, so its maximum scales as exp(Δr/B_w): a radius
smaller by 0.025 m lowers g_max by the factor exp(-0.025/0.08) = 0.732, the computed 0.7316. The drive does not
depend on r, so the stuck-alone fraction is the fraction of agents with m v0/tau below g_max(r), and it falls
where the drive distribution's lower tail meets the shifted g_max. The SFM keeps 8.1 stuck-alone agents per
100-agent run; one stuck-alone agent among the last arrivals suffices for a lone trap (B2: held agents arrived with
at most 9 others active), so the lone trap should survive the smaller radius in the SFM, at a lower deadlock
frequency that this item does not measure. Force-coupled ORCA's rest drive is about twice the SFM's; its stuck-alone fraction falls by a factor of 8 to
0.16 agents per run, so at mu_r = 0.225 m about 15% of 100-agent runs (1 - (1 - 0.0016)^100) contain one agent
that force-coupled ORCA cannot free alone.

**CLAIM (scope).** The stuck-alone fraction is set by the radius distribution as much as by the wall range: with the
mean radius at 0.225 m the SFM fraction is 0.081 instead of 0.23 and force-coupled ORCA's 0.0016 instead of 0.013.
Any sentence that transfers the fraction (or the B2 critical width, which follows from g_max(r)) to the CroMa
geometry or to human body sizes needs the radius stated. The qualitative result (SFM holds lone agents at
w = 0.8 m, force-coupled ORCA frees nearly all) holds at both radii.
