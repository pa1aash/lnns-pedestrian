# A10: replay of the noise quench with the noise stream held fixed

Scripts: `experiments/revision2/A10_noise_replay.py` (runner; queue step A10 in `experiments/revision2/queue_rev2.yaml`,
4 workers, estimate 0.5 CPU-h) and `experiments/revision2/A10_analyze.py` (analysis, runnable once
`data/revision2/A10/a10.jsonl` is complete). Output `data/derived/rev2_A10.json`; registry `notes/C2_items/A10.yaml`
(prefix `RvAten`). Until the data exist, assemble the registry with `build_registry2.py A10`.
**Status: complete** (340 runs, 0.21 CPU-h, queue step A10 on 2026-11-12).

## Question

A7's replay probe separates noise from force-coupled ORCA (RVO2) at the lone keystone (9:0 changing snapshots for E2,
14:0 for N1) and is marked definitional: the noise side compares independent seeds. A10 asks whether the noise
quench is sensitive to a 1e-9 m displacement when the noise sequence is the same, i.e. whether the replay signature
measures the dynamics or only the change of noise stream.

## What was done

- Snapshots: the 34 lone keystones of E2 (`data/derived/selection.json`, class `lone_keystone`, selection order
  k = 0..33), restored with `w2lib.restore`; blocker from the selection.
- Quench: C1 + CF flags, per-step velocity noise sigma = 0.07 m/s, noise seed 7000 + 100 k + rep (rep 0-4), dt = 0.01 s,
  full 120 s window (`runlib.quench`, as E2).
- Each quench twice: reference (as E2) and replay (every residual agent displaced by 1e-9 m Gaussian,
  `np.random.default_rng(900 + rep)`, as `runlib.quench` does for the deterministic replays) with the same noise seed.
  The noise comes from the simulation's own generator, seeded by the noise seed; the displacement uses a separate
  generator, so the two runs draw the same noise sequence (they can diverge only in which agents the draws act on once
  an exit order changes).
- Reference check: every reference run must reproduce the stored E2 record bit for bit (exits, final positions,
  t_run). Verified in the smoke test on the two full-length references of E1b_seed52 (rep 0 and 1).
- Outcome: blocker freed within 120 s, and its exit time. Flip = freed in one run of the pair and not the other.
- Tests: run level, exact McNemar on (reference-only, replay-only) flips over 170 pairs, Wilson interval of the flip
  fraction, and the independent-seed baseline (chance that two different E2 seeds of a snapshot disagree,
  mean of 2k(5-k)/20). Snapshot level, a snapshot changes when any of its five pairs flips (A7's rule for
  deterministic replays), compared by exact McNemar with (i) the ordinary replays of force-coupled ORCA (RVO2)
  (and of every deterministic E2 arm, in the JSON) and (ii) the independent-seed changes (A7's 9 snapshots).

Smoke test: `--limit 2 --scale 0.05 --workers 1` to the scratchpad, plus 4 full-length jobs: both references match
E2; on E1b_seed52 rep 0 the replay frees the blocker at the same step as the reference (12.60 s).
Cost: E2's 170 noise runs at sigma = 0.07 took 0.21 CPU-h (4.4 s mean), so 340 runs take about 0.42 CPU-h.

## Results

All 170 references reproduce E2 bit for bit. The blocker is freed in 148 of 170 references and in the same 148
replays; every one of the 148 exits at the identical time step (shift 0 s). Flips: 0 of 170 (95% interval up to
0.022), against a 0.15 chance that two independent noise seeds of the same snapshot disagree. Snapshot level:
0 of 34 changed by the fixed-stream replay, 9 by independent seeds (McNemar 0:9, p = 0.0039), 0 by the replays of
force-coupled ORCA (RVO2).

## Interpretation

Two outcomes are possible. If the fixed-stream replay flips about as often as independent seeds disagree, the noise
quench is chaotic at the 1e-9 m scale, the replay probe measures the dynamics, and A7's "definitional" label for the
noise side can be dropped. If it flips rarely (close to the 0 of ORCA's replays), the replay difference between noise
and force-coupled ORCA comes from the change of noise stream alone. Then the probe separates a stochastic surrogate
from a deterministic one by construction, which the paper should state. The data give the second outcome without exception:
at sigma = 0.07 m/s the noise (velocity kicks of order 7e-4 m per step) swamps a 1e-9 m displacement, so the
trajectory is fixed by the noise sequence. The noise arm's replay sensitivity is the change of noise stream.

**CLAIM**: Sect. 4.1/Table 2 treat "replay" as a signature of fluctuation-driven escape. The fixed-stream flip count is zero, so the sentence must say that the replay signature of noise is the seed-to-seed change, and the
replay probe only tells a stochastic perturbation from a deterministic one.
