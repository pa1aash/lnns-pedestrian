# A3: censoring-aware Arrhenius slope of the lone blocker over β = 1.5–7, and why it sits below 1

Scripts: `experiments/revision2/A3_arrhenius.py` (analysis) -> `data/derived/rev2_A3.json`;
`experiments/revision2/A3_toy_runs.py` + `A3_toylib.py` (toy runs) -> `data/revision2/A3/toy.json`.
Registry `notes/C2_items/A3.yaml` (prefix `RvAthree`). CPU: toy 17 min, analysis about 6 min, one process.

## Data and method
- **Data.** Three alone snapshots: E1b_seed72, E1b_seed162 and E1b_seed192, with ΔU_alone = 5.8, 21 and 56 J. The levels are β = 1.5, 2.5, 3.5 (E4) and β = 5, 6, 7 (B3), with 20 restarts per cell: 360 restarts, 321 escapes, 39 censored.
- **Censoring-aware fit.** Under a constant rate per cell, the right-censored likelihood is λ^d e^{−λE}, where d is the number of escapes and E the total time at risk. The fit is therefore a Poisson regression of d with offset ln E. The model is ln λ = a_snapshot − r β. Because β = ΔU/k_BT_eff exactly, r is the fitted slope over the string barrier. The fit is pooled: one r and three intercepts, maximum likelihood.
- **Intervals.** 95% intervals come from a bootstrap of the restarts within each cell (2000 replicates). This keeps each restart's censoring and does not assume exponential times. The profile-likelihood interval is also given.
- **Sensitivity fit.** A censored Weibull MLE per cell gives the mean time θΓ(1+1/k). Its log is regressed on β with event weights and the same bootstrap.
- **Weibull shapes.** Censored profile likelihood (`survival.weibull_pool`): censored restarts count as survivors to their horizon. One shape per snapshot over β = 6–7, with a scale per cell.
- **Causes.**
  - (a) Finite barrier. Fits with an extra common term c ln β or c/β. A no-free-parameter rate for the same potential: the exact 2-D transition-state rate of the string potential U (anharmonic well and saddle line integrated on a 4 mm grid), times the Kramers factor κ and the Mel'nikov–Meshkov energy-diffusion factor A(Δ), with Δ = γS/k_BT and S the axial action at the saddle energy.
  - (b) Discrete-time temperature. Derived from the AR(1) map of the code's update.
  - (c) The speed cap 2v₀ and the barrier end points.
  - **Toy.** The blocker alone in U, with the code's fixed-numerics update (v' = clamp(v + Δt(F/m − v/τ)), noise, re-clamp, x' = x + Δt v'). 300 walkers per cell at all six levels, plus no-cap and Δt = 0.0025 s variants at β ≤ 3.5.

### Further checks (not in the registry)
- **Barrier end points.** The string, climbing-image and 1-D axis barriers differ by at most 0.8% of ΔU (5.8 J) and by 0.004% (56 J).
- **Theory against data.** The theoretical rates lie within a factor 0.54–1.6 of the measured ones, with no fitted constant.
- **Toy Δt variant.** At N = 300 the Δt = 0.0025 s factors (0.96–1.29) are dominated by sampling noise, so the analytic result in (b) stands for the Δt effect.

## Interpretation
- **The pooled ratio.** Over β = 1.5–7 the pooled censoring-aware ratio is 0.89 [0.83, 0.95]. The shortfall is real at this precision.
- **Censoring and dispersion.** The sensitivity fits that allow for non-exponential times (bootstrap, Weibull means) give the same ratio. So the shortfall comes neither from censoring nor from over-dispersion.
- **What the shortfall is.** It is the finite-barrier curvature of ln λ(β). The slope reaches ΔU only as β → ∞.
  - The well is bounded upstream by the linear drive term c|x − g|. It is softer than harmonic, and more so the deeper the barrier. As a result, the well's configurational integral grows faster than T, and the transition-state prefactor rises with β.
  - At β = 7 this prefactor is still only 0.63 of its harmonic limit for the 56 J trap, against 0.98 for the 5.8 J trap.
  - At the lowest levels, energy-diffusion depletion adds to this (A = 0.81–0.84 at β = 1.5; γ/ω ≈ 0.5, an under-damped well).
- **Theory and toy.** The transition-state rate of the same potential, with no free parameter, predicts 0.90 pooled and the per-snapshot order 0.95 > 0.89 > 0.84. The toy, which has only the string potential and the code's update, gives 0.91 [0.89, 0.93] and 0.98, 0.89, 0.84. The production runs (0.90, 0.93, 0.85) match both. So the other force terms (ORCA, TTC, corner rule) and the snapshot start play no part in the shortfall.
- **Rejected: the discrete-time bias.** The scheme is semi-implicit, and its Euler–Maruyama configurational temperature is T_eff(1 − γΔt/2): 0.8% colder, curvature-independent to first order (h²ω²/4 ≤ 10⁻³ here). It raises the ratio by 0.8% and so cannot explain a shortfall.
- **The speed cap.** It cuts the deep trap's kinetic temperature to 0.81 T_eff at β = 1.5 (2v₀ = 1.6 m/s against a thermal speed of 0.68 m/s). Its rate effect there is a factor 1.2. Removing it moves the pooled ratio by only +0.006, and it is absent at β ≥ 2.5.
- **Fits with a curvature term.** Adding a c/β or c ln β term moves r to 0.94 or 0.97. The interval then covers 1, but six levels per snapshot cannot pin c (its interval covers 0). The theory, not the extra fit term, identifies the cause.
- **Open: the Weibull shape at 56 J.** The over-dispersion at 56 J, β = 6–7 (k = 0.66 [0.49, 0.87], 32 escapes) is not reproduced by the toy (k = 1.0 [0.90, 1.1], 249 escapes). It comes from something the toy lacks, or it is a sampling fluctuation. It does not affect r: the Weibull-mean fit agrees.

## **CLAIM**
- **The statement that changes.** The paper's statement that the Arrhenius slope equals the string barrier (ratio 1) should be replaced. The finite-β slope is 0.89 [0.83, 0.95] of the string barrier, as predicted (0.90) by the transition-state rate of the same potential. The harmonic value 1 is the β → ∞ limit.
- **What this supports.** It supports the string barrier as the activation energy, with a quantified finite-barrier correction. It does not support any missing physics or a mismatch of the noise mapping.
- **Relation to B3.** This supersedes B3's "one common offset below 1": the offset is the prefactor's β-dependence, and it grows with ΔU through the well's anharmonicity.
