# A2 Damping regime of the lone-trap escapes: Kramers–Grote–Hynes and Mel'nikov–Meshkov factors

Script `experiments/revision2/A2_damping.py` writes `data/derived/rev2_A2.json`. Registry entries are in `notes/C2_items/A2.yaml` (prefix RvAtwo). No dynamics was run; the analysis took 64–82 s of CPU per run in one process.

## What was done

**Barriers.** The 10 E4 snapshots in three conditions: full, pinned (`frozen`) and alone. The barrier field is U of `experiments/BAR/bar.py`. The minimum comes from `bar.relax`, as in `fix_a3`; the saddle is the gate's accepted climbing image. E1b_seed60 full has ΔU = 0.02 J and is skipped, as in E4. That leaves 29 barriers. 28 of them have an E4 Arrhenius fit; E1b_seed170 full (0.13 J) has none.

**Dynamics.** Near rest the code integrates

  m ẍ = −∇U − (m/τ_i) ẋ + noise,

with Euler steps of Δt = 0.01 s. Every agent has m = 80 kg, so the mass-weighted Hessian is H/m.

**Formulas used.**
- Hessians: central second differences of U, h = 1e-5 m, at the minimum and at the saddle. The unstable eigenvalue λ_u agrees with `fix_a3.json` to all printed digits.
- Barrier frequency: ω_b = √(|λ_u|/m).
- Well frequency: ω_a = √(tᵀH_min t/m) along the path tangent t at the minimum. The softest-mode value √(μ₁/m) is also stored.
- Friction: γ = 1/τ_b of the blocker. Also γ_u = Σ_i p_i/τ_i, where p_i are the agents' weights in the unstable eigenvector. γ_u differs from γ by at most 12%, and only where the blocker does not carry the mode.
- Kramers–Grote–Hynes factor in the spatial-diffusion (Markovian) form:

  κ = √(1 + (γ/2ω_b)²) − γ/(2ω_b).

  The multidimensional form is λ₊/ω_b, where λ₊ is the Langer growth rate, the positive root of det(λ²m I + λ diag(m/τ_i) + H_sad) = 0.
- Langer rate (from `fix_a3`): ν_L = (λ₊/2π) √(det H_min / |det H_sad|), so k_Langer = ν_L e^(−β) already contains κ. The transition-state value is ν_TST = (ω_b/2π) √(det H_min / |det H_sad|).
- Undamped action at the barrier energy E_b = U_sad, approximated along a one-dimensional path:

  S(E_b) = 2 ∫ √(2m (E_b − U(l))) dl,

  integrated from the inner turning point to the saddle.
  - Uphill part: the minimum-energy path from the saddle to the minimum, by gradient flow Ẋ = −∇U. This is mass-weighted steepest descent, since the masses are equal. The flow is started 1e-4 m along the unstable eigenvector, takes steps α = 0.5/λ_max(H_sad) capped at 1 mm per agent, and stops at U − U_min < 1e-4 ΔU.
  - Far side of the minimum: the straight line from the minimum in the direction −t, out to U = E_b.
  - Approximation stated: the orbit is taken on this one path and transverse motion is neglected.
  - Checks: the harmonic value S_h = 2πΔU/ω_a and the mirror value (twice the uphill part). S/S_h lies between \RvAtwoSHarmMin and \RvAtwoSHarmMax. The ratio is about 1.1 for single-agent fields, because the drive term makes the well's far side linear rather than parabolic. It reaches 3.5 for two multi-agent full paths.
- Energy loss per oscillation, in units of k_BT:

  Δ = γ S(E_b)/k_BT, with k_BT = ΔU/β, so Δ = (γS/ΔU) β.

  A variant, Δ_path, projects the friction onto the path tangent: γ_t = Σ_k t_k²/τ_k. It differs from Δ by less than 1% in the result.
- Mel'nikov–Meshkov / Pollak–Grabert–Hänggi depopulation factor:

  A(Δ) = exp[(1/π) ∫₀^∞ ln(1 − e^(−Δ(x² + 1/4))) dx/(x² + 1/4)].

  This equals (1/2π) times the integral over the whole real line; the brief's (1/π) applies to the half line. The limits are checked numerically: A(0.001) = 0.00097 ≈ Δ, and A(30) = 0.9999. The turnover rate is k = A(Δ) k_Langer. A is therefore the prediction for the measured ν̂/ν_L, and κA is the prediction for ν̂/ν_TST.
- Levels: β = 1.5, 2.5, 3.5 (E4, all conditions) and β = 5, 6, 7 (B3, alone, 3 snapshots).
  - Comparison with ν̂: ν̂ holds the slope at −ΔU and is the event-weighted mean of ln λ + β over the levels. The matching prediction is A_fit = exp(Σ_L d_L ln A(Δ_L) / Σ_L d_L).
  - Per cell: the measured ratio is λ_L e^(β_L)/ν_L.
- Sensitivity: A recomputed with S_low, the smallest of the three action estimates.

Numerical checks:
- The gradient-flow path ends within 0.01 J of the relaxed minimum at 28 of 29 barriers. In E1b_seed237 full it reaches a minimum 0.046 J lower than `bar.relax`'s, which is 0.4% of ΔU.
- The far-side line reaches E_b at every barrier.
- ω_b Δt ≤ 0.067, so the Euler step resolves the barrier crossing.

## Interpretation

The blocker is underdamped at the saddle. The friction 1/τ is 0.27–0.56 of the barrier frequency in the pinned and alone fields and in most full fields. Only three full saddles exceed 1: E1b_seed61, E1b_seed158 and E1b_seed170, where the neighbours make the saddle soft (ω_b = 0.83–1.9 s⁻¹). The spatial-diffusion factor κ = 0.82 (median) is therefore a 20% correction, and the Langer prefactor ν_L of the paper already contains it through λ₊. The 1-D and multidimensional values agree to 0.04.

The energy-diffusion limit does not bind at any level that was run. The energy the blocker loses to friction in one undamped oscillation at the barrier energy is γS ≈ 4.5 ΔU, so Δ ≈ 4.5 β ≥ 3.4 already at β = 1.5. Friction refills the high-energy states near the barrier much faster than escape depletes them. The Mel'nikov–Meshkov factor is accordingly between 0.79 and 1.0 over the E4 cells and 0.997–1.0 at β = 5–7. The turnover (Δ ≈ 1) would need β ≈ 0.22, which lies below even the fraction-matched noise (median β 0.64, A3). Kramers–Langer theory with the turnover correction thus predicts ν̂/ν_L ≈ 0.97 (A_fit, median), and κA_fit ≈ 0.79 relative to transition-state theory.

The measured median 0.69 lies outside the range the factors predict: below A's minimum of 0.79 over all E4 cells, and below 0.76 with the smallest action estimate. None of the 28 fits falls inside its own predicted interval. The turnover accounts for about 3 percentage points of the 31% deficit; the remaining factor, measured/A_fit, has median 0.71.

The deficit is not a damping effect, and it is not an artefact of holding the slope at −ΔU. The free-slope intercept gives 0.61, and ν̂/ν_L does not correlate with the slope ratio (ρ = 0.018). It persists at β = 5–7 (median 0.83, cell scatter 0.46–1.4), where the turnover correction is below 0.3%. The data here leave two candidate sources open:
- The anharmonic far side of the well. The drive makes it linear, and S/S_h reaches 3.5 for the multi-agent paths.
- Finite-barrier corrections at β ≤ 3.5.

This analysis does not separate the two.

## Paper claims
- **CLAIM** (wording, Sect. 5.2 / Appendix on the Langer comparison). The paper places the escapes in the Kramers–Langer regime and leaves the 0.69 open. The regime is now established: underdamped at the saddle (γ/ω_b median 0.41), but well above the energy-diffusion turnover (Δ ≥ 3.4). The 0.69 must not be attributed to damping or to energy-diffusion depopulation; those predict 0.88–1.0. If the text explains the deficit by "intermediate damping" or "Kramers turnover", that explanation goes.
- No change to the numbers FixAthreeNuRatioAll (0.69) or the Arrhenius ratios.
