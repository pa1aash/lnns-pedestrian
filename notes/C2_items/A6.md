# A6 Split-half fit of the lateral bias b*

Script `experiments/revision2/A6_bstar_split.py` -> `data/derived/rev2_A6.json`; registry `notes/C2_items/A6.yaml`
(prefix `RvAsix`). Existing data only, no new runs, 2 s CPU, one process.

## What was done

- **Data.** 46 pair standoffs, 120 s quench, unperturbed runs. ORCA: `C3_pub` (legacy, 21 freed) and `C3_rvo2`
  (RVO2, 7 freed) in `data/E7b/e7b_quench.jsonl`. Per-snapshot static fold thresholds b*_i (sign +1):
  `data/E7b_bstar/fold_threshold.jsonl`.
- **Fitting rule** (`experiments/E7b/matched_b.py fit`, unchanged, applied to a half S of 23 snapshots). k = ORCA's
  freed count on S; b*_S = midpoint of the k-th and (k+1)-th smallest thresholds of S (0 below the first). The fit
  uses the static thresholds only, so it needs no simulation. On all 46 snapshots the rule reproduces
  `bstar.json` (b*_legacy = \RvAsixLegacyBFull = 0.02234, b*_RVO2 = \RvAsixRvoBFull = 0.00906; asserted).
- **Prediction on the held-out half H.** The bias arm was simulated at eight values of b:
  0 (the SFM), 0.00906 (`bstar_rvo2+`), 0.01, 0.02, 0.02234 (`bstar_pub+`), 0.03, 0.05, 0.1 (`bias<b>`), and frees
  0, 8, 8, 17, 22, 32, 44, 46 standoffs. Every snapshot's quench outcome is monotone in b on this grid
  (\RvAsixNNonmono\ exceptions). The held-out freed fraction at b*_S is the mean over H of the per-snapshot 0/1
  outcome, linearly interpolated between the two grid values that bracket b*_S (primary). The static prediction
  #{i ∈ H : b*_i ≤ b*_S}/23 is given beside; it disagrees with the quench on 1 of 46 snapshots at three grid points
  (0.00906, 0.02, 0.02234) and on none at the others.
- **Held-out error.** e = predicted bias freed fraction on H − ORCA's freed fraction on H.
- **Splits.** 200 random permutations from `Generator(PCG64(2026))`; the first 23 snapshots fit, the last 23 are
  held out; the same splits for both solvers.
- **Reference.** The split alone: ORCA's freed fraction on H minus that on S. A predictor that reproduces the fitting
  half exactly and transfers perfectly would still show this error.

## Interpretation

- The fitted bias is stable: b*_S stays within ±20% of the full-sample value in 95% of splits (legacy). The fit
  reproduces the fitting half to within 0.09.
- The held-out error is unbiased (legacy median +0.044, mean +0.016; one standoff of 23 is 0.043) and as wide as the
  split alone: for the legacy solver the RMS error (0.16) equals the RMS difference between ORCA's freed fractions
  on two random halves (0.16). The rule therefore transfers to unseen standoffs; its error is the sampling error of
  ORCA's own freed fraction on 23 standoffs.
- With RVO2 ORCA frees 7 of 46, and a half that holds 5–7 of them pushes b*_S up to 0.02, onto the steep part of the
  freed curve (8 freed at b = 0.01, 17 at 0.02). The held-out error then has a positive tail (97.5th percentile
  +0.40 against +0.22 for the split alone; RMS 0.15 against 0.11). The bias reproduces a small ORCA fraction with less
  transfer than a fraction near one half.
- About half the splits miss ORCA's held-out fraction by more than 0.10; on 23 standoffs this is the expected spread
  and agrees with A5, where the full-sample bias is not resolved to ±0.10 by 46 standoffs.

## Claims

- **CLAIM** (Sect. 6.3, "reproduces each solver's freed fraction at T = 120 s by construction"). Add the out-of-sample
  check: fitted on half the standoffs, b* predicts the other half's freed fraction with a median error of +0.04 and
  a 95% range of [−0.29, 0.30] (legacy), the range of the split alone. No change of class; the bias match carries
  a sampling error of ±0.3 at 23 standoffs.
