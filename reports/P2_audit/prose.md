# Prose audit: "Mechanism Screening for Deadlock Resolution in a Social Force Model"

Standard: `lnns-launch/waveA/prose_prompt.md` (16 categories). Rulings in force: vocabulary v3+v4, R6 (three classes plus defect flag), R11 (scope of "activated escape"), R12 (throttle), the fixed final sentence of the Conclusions, no new numbers. Every replacement below uses only numbers already in the text or macros already in `paper/numbers.tex`.

**Anchors.** `file:line` refers to `paper/sections/<file>`, `paper/tables/<file>` or `figures/fig1_caption.tex`. Most paragraphs are one source line, so the quoted text locates the sentence. Printed values are from `paper/main.pdf` as built at 12:11 on 12 Nov. Lines starting `%C2:` and the `\pending` back-matter placeholders are excluded.

**Severity.** H: a skeptical reviewer will catch it. M: costs the reader time. L: polish. **[taste]**: a style preference rather than a defect; the competing convention is named.

**Highest-leverage entries, in order:** 13.1, 13.9, 13.12, 13.5, 8.12/13.4, 14.2 with 9.1, 14.1, 3.7 with 9.10, 12 (rows 1, 2, 9, 16), 13.6, 8.8.

The prose already avoids most of the standard's tics: it has no significance announcements, no "What" clefts and almost no softening adverbs. Its defects are density, ordering, terminology drift and some table/text inconsistencies. The worst paragraph is the Arrhenius paragraph of Sect. 5.2 (`05_deadlock_physics.tex:42`): nine sentences, more than 30 numbers, three conditions, and an explanation (the eight full-condition fits) that arrives in the next paragraph. Its closest competitor is the snapshot-set paragraph of Sect. 3.4 (`03_pedestrian_model.tex:67–68`).
