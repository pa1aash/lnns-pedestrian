## Worst sentence and worst paragraph in the range

**Worst sentence: line 1120 [06], the last sentence of "The second trap and the published solver."**

> Only the published linear program changes outcome under $10^{-9}$\,m replays (\EtwoReplayCThreePubFlips\ of \EtwoReplayN, exit times shifted by up to \EtwoReplayCThreePubMaxDt\,s, all at the second trap in slow creep), which a replay this small reveals as ill-conditioning of that implementation (class N) and base forward claims on RVO2.

This sentence carries the class-N verdict, and it has four problems. A three-item parenthetical holds an undefined term ("slow creep"). The "which" clause takes the whole preceding clause as its antecedent. The coordinated second verb "base" has no subject: as written, "which" both "reveals" and "base[s]". And "forward claims" is a project-internal phrase (CHANGELOG C20). Split:

> Only the published linear program changes outcome under $10^{-9}$\,m replays: 5 of 300, all at the second trap while the agents creep slowly toward the door, with exit times shifted by up to 80.8\,s. An outcome that a $10^{-9}$\,m shift can change marks ill-conditioning of that implementation (class N), and we take RVO2 as the reference ORCA implementation.

Two sentences compete for the title. One is line 1151 [07], the 75-word estimator sentence ("Model and human trajectories went through the same estimator: …", entry 9.15). The other is line 1177 [09], sentence 5 ("Controls matched to ORCA's rate, noise at the lone trap and in whole runs and a lateral bias at the pair, reproduce …", entry 9.28).

**Worst paragraph: line 1120 [06]**, at 175 words. It holds three run-on sentences (entries 9.6, 9.7, 4.1), an ambiguous pronoun ("from its half-plane", 16.3), two undefined terms ("slow creep", "forward claims") and two topics under one heading (7.3).
