# A11: the constraint part of force-coupled ORCA at rest

Script: `experiments/revision/A11_orca_constraint.py` -> `data/derived/rev_A11.json` (registry `notes/A11.yaml`, prefix `RevAeleven`); per-agent rows in `data/revision/A11/static.jsonl`, relaxations in `data/revision/A11/relax.jsonl`. CPU about 8 min, one process (rest-field relaxations; the static parts take seconds). Rerun the summaries without relaxing: `--reuse`.

## Definitions
- **Snapshots and flags.** The 46 pair snapshots use CF + B_w = 0.04. The 26 E2 second-trap snapshots use CF.
- **Static split.** All velocities are set to 0, and the neighbour lists and densities are those of `Simulation.step`. v_ORCA comes from the code's solver (`_velocity_rvo2` / `_velocity_published`) with v_pref = v0 ê. Then:
  - drive part D = w_o m v_pref/τ_orca;
  - constraint part C = w_o m (v_ORCA − v_pref)/τ_orca, which is exactly the rest value of the `constraint_only` arm.
- **Decomposition of C.** C is projected onto ê, onto e⊥ = (−e_y, e_x), and onto the lateral unit vector pointing towards the axis y = 5. Its angle to the unit vector n towards the partner is reported. "Binds" means |v_ORCA − v_pref| > 1e-9 m/s.
- **Cap prediction.** At zero relative velocity the ORCA line is a cap line: v·n ≤ s, with slack s = (d − r_ij)/(2τ_h). It predicts C = −w_o m (v_pref·n − s) n/τ_orca.
- **Rest-field relaxation.** Overdamped flow dx/dt = (τ/m) F(x, v = 0), velocities held at zero, Euler step 0.01 s up to 120 s (as `pairlib.relax`). Arms: C1, drive_only, constraint_only and C3 for both solvers. Freed means a member, or the second-trap blocker, reaches x > 10.3 m.
- **Common-mode load.** The 4-vector force is projected on the pair's softest Hessian mode v1 (E7b_static). It is expressed as an equivalent lateral bias b_eq = (F·v1)/(Σ c_i e⊥_i·v1_i) and compared with the static fold threshold b*_s of the same sign (`data/E7b_bstar/fold_threshold.jsonl`).
- **Jacobian check.** J = −∂F0/∂x of the ORCA rest field at the snapshot, by central differences (h = 1e-5 m), using `A10_hessian.RestField`.

## Results: pair standoffs (92 members)
- **The constraint binds at rest at every member: 92/92**, both solvers.
  - Its rest value is the same for the two solvers to 7e-14 N. At v = 0 every line is a cap line, and the solvers differ only on leg lines.
  - v_pref points towards the partner at 0.16–0.47 m/s, against a slack s = 0.028–0.033 m/s.
  - The cap prediction holds to 7e-14 N. C is exactly anti-parallel to n (cos = −1 in 92/92) and removes a median 0.90 of v_pref's partner-ward component.
- **Size and direction of C.**
  - |C| = 20–70 N (median 43), which is 0.19–0.35 (median 0.27) of the drive |D| (median 165 N; w_o ≥ 0.9996).
  - Because the members stand abreast (partner x offset ≤ 0.075 m), C is lateral and points away from the axis: median 41 N outward. That cancels a median 0.86 of the drive's axis-ward component (49 N).
  - Its door-ward component is |C_x| ≤ 5.9 N, so the rest-state cancellation −C_x/D_x lies within ±0.03.
  - Relative to ê, C has an axial component of −13 N (median). That comes only from ê's tilt towards the axis.
- **Held vs freed under force-coupled ORCA.** C does not separate them. RVO2: held |C| median 42 N (ratio 0.27) against freed 49 N (0.26). Legacy solver: similar.
- **Consistency with the trace data.** My static C_x reproduces `e7b_pair_trace` cons_x_t0 to 1.7e-4 N. PairTraceCancel (0.26) and StHeldCancel (0.27) are time means of −C_x/D_x over the 20 s runs, while the members move. At t = 0 the same quantity is 0.00. The door-ward opposition therefore builds up in motion; it is not a rest-state property.
- **Rest-field relaxation (pairs; freed count, then agreement with the quench):**

  | Arm | Relaxation frees | Quench frees | Agree |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | C1 | 0/46 | 0 | 46/46 |
  | drive_only | 31 | 33 | 44 |
  | constraint_only (RVO2) | 46 | 44 | 44 |
  | constraint_only (legacy) | 46 | 45 | 45 |
  | C3 (RVO2) | 25 | 7 | 28 |
  | C3 (legacy) | 25 | 21 | 32 |

  - The rest field alone accounts for the drive-only and constraint-only outcomes.
  - For force-coupled ORCA (RVO2), the rest field frees 25. Of those, 18 are held in the quench, and none is freed in the quench that the relaxation holds.
  - Of the 28 standoffs that the drive part frees and ORCA (RVO2) holds, 13 are held already by the rest field, and 15 are held only dynamically.
  - The solver difference (RVO2 7 against legacy 21) is entirely dynamic, since the rest fields are identical.
- **Why the constraint part alone frees.**
  1. *It loads the soft common lateral mode.* The two members are pushed apart by unequal amounts, which acts as an equivalent bias |b_eq| of median 0.033, against b*_f median 0.023. The common-mode load exceeds the fold threshold in 30/46 snapshots. Its sign predicts which member exits first in 44/46 relaxations. The ORCA drive's own common-mode load is smaller (|b_eq| median 0.013).
  2. *It removes the stiffness of that mode.* C depends on position through n and through v_pref·n. The symmetric part of the constraint-only rest-field Jacobian is not positive definite in 45/46 snapshots (softest eigenvalue median −19 N/m, against μ1 = 94 N/m for SFM). The constraint part therefore contributes about −110 N/m on the soft mode, so the field has no minimum near the snapshot. The remaining 16 snapshots are freed for this reason even though their load is below b*_f.
- **Why adding the drive frees fewer standoffs than the drive alone.**
  - At rest, the ORCA drive's goal-directed curvature restores stiffness: the drive-only symmetric softest eigenvalue is 195 N/m (median). With drive and constraint together (C3) the field is positive definite in 46/46 (softest eigenvalue 54–104 N/m, median 79).
  - The constraint part also cancels most of the drive's axis-ward squeeze.
  - Together these lower the rest-field yield from 31 to 25. The quench drop from 33 to 7 (RVO2) is mostly dynamic: the half-plane against the partner turns door-ward once the members move, giving the 0.26 time-mean cancellation.
- **Non-conservative rest field.** The ORCA rest field is not a gradient: ‖J − Jᵀ‖/‖J‖ = 0.08–0.10 (median 0.09) for C3, against 0 for drive_only.

## Results: second traps (26)
- **The blocker's constraint binds at rest in 9/26.** These are the abreast geometries of A10. In each of the 9 it is anti-parallel to n and lateral, pointing away from the axis.
- **Binding at rest separates the outcomes.** It occurs in 8 of the 9 blockers that ORCA (RVO2) holds and in 1 of the 17 it frees (Fisher p = 4.9e-5). In held runs |C|/|D| has median 0.23.
- **The other residual agents are held back strongly at rest.** Their constraint parts are large and point backward, with an axial component of −82 N median (their half-planes are against the blocker ahead).
- **Rest-field relaxation:**
  - drive_only frees 24 (quench 24; agree 26/26);
  - constraint_only frees 0 (4 undecided; quench 0 RVO2 and 1 legacy);
  - C3 frees 19 (quench 17 RVO2; agree 22/26).
  - Of the 7 blockers freed by the drive part but held by ORCA (RVO2), 4 are held already by the rest field.
- **Why the constraint part alone frees none here.** With the default wall range (B_w = 0.08) each agent is held alone. The outward lateral load does not open the trap.

## Claims this changes (quoted)
1. Sect. 3.2: "At rest with no binding constraint v^orca_i = v0 ê_i, and Eq. (orca) gives ORCA's rest drive". At every pair member, and at 9/26 second-trap blockers, the constraint binds at rest. The rest-state ORCA force there is D + C, and C is 0.19–0.35 of D and lateral. The statement is true only for agents without an abreast neighbour.
2. Sect. 4.3: "Part (ii) does not cover ORCA's velocity-dependent constraint part." The constraint part has a position-dependent rest value, so it is part of F0. The resulting ORCA rest field is non-conservative (asymmetry 0.08–0.10). Part (ii) therefore fails for force-coupled ORCA at the pair already at rest, not only through the velocity dependence. Suggested wording below.
3. Sect. 6.4.1: "Added to the drive part, the constraint part opposes it". At rest the constraint part has no door-ward component: it points away from the partner and cancels the drive's lateral squeeze. The opposition to the door-ward drive arises in motion. Of the 28 discordant standoffs (drive frees, RVO2 holds), 13 follow from the rest field and 15 are dynamic.
4. Sect. 6.1.4, "In held runs the constraint part from the neighbour's half-plane cancels on average \StHeldCancel\ of the drive part". This is consistent as a time mean over the 20 s trace. At the restart the cancellation is 0.00, so the sentence should say "while the blocker moves".
5. The velocity-coupling slack statement (Sect. 6.6) is confirmed (s = 0.03 m/s). Under force coupling, however, v_pref exceeds that slack in every pair member.
