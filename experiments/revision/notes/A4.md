# A4 Trend of the Arrhenius slope ratio with the barrier (Fig. 4 right; the referee's "Fig. 5 right")

Script: `experiments/revision/A4_ratio_trend.py` -> `data/derived/rev_A4.json`; registry `notes/A4.yaml`.
The figure in question is `figures/fig4_escape.pdf`, right panel (Fig. 4 in the current paper).

## What was done
- Data: `data/derived/E4_analysis.json` (experiments/E4/analyze.py), the 28 (snapshot, condition) Arrhenius
  fits on 10 snapshots (full 8, pinned 10, alone 10). r = −s/ΔU_cond, s the event-weighted LS slope of ln λ̂ on
  1/k_BT_eff over the levels β = 1.5, 2.5, 3.5; per-fit 95% interval from Poisson resampling of the counts.
- Regressor x = ln(ΔU_cond/J), ΔU_cond the barrier of that condition (the abscissa of the figure).
- Models: per condition r = a_c + b_c x; pooled with condition intercepts r = a_c + b x; pooled with a common
  intercept r = a + b x.
- Intervals for b: (i) OLS with a bootstrap over snapshots (4000 draws, each snapshot carries all its fits);
  (ii) WLS with weights 1/se², se = (hi − lo)/3.92 from the per-fit interval, interval from the residual-scaled
  covariance (t, dof) and the unscaled known-variance interval (z); (iii) a parametric Poisson bootstrap of every
  cell count with all ratios refitted (measurement error only).
- Checks: leave-one-snapshot-out range; Spearman correlation; refit with the realised blocker temperature
  (per-cell velocity variance from the stored moments of data/E4/e4_escape.jsonl, c = m var/k_BT_eff,
  median 0.99, range 0.75–1.10; abscissa 1/(c k_BT_eff)).

## Interpretation
Pooled over the three conditions, with one intercept per condition, the ratio shows no trend with ΔU (b = −0.001,
snapshot-bootstrap interval [−0.06, 0.06], i.e. at most ±0.15 per decade of ΔU). The common-intercept fit
gives a weak negative slope whose interval covers 0 by bootstrap and only marginally excludes it by known-variance
WLS; that slope comes from the condition offsets, not from a within-condition trend shared by all three.
Within the alone condition the trend is clear and survives every interval method: the deepest one-agent barriers
(40–56 J, crossed at k_BT_eff = 11–37 J) give r ≈ 0.6–0.75, so the noise crosses them over an effective barrier
of 0.6–0.75 of the string barrier. The pinned condition points the same way, weakly.

What a trend can and cannot mean: every fit uses the same three β levels, so a prefactor ν ∝ (k_BT)^α
(Langer/Kramers corrections, entropic widening) and the low-β saturation of the Kramers rate both shift r by an
amount set by β alone (r = 1 + α⟨1/β⟩, ⟨1/β⟩ = 0.47), the same for every ΔU. They can explain a common offset of r
from 1 (pooled median ≈ 0.95) but not a trend in ΔU. A ΔU-dependent r means the computed barrier misstates the
dynamically relevant one by a ΔU-dependent factor. For the alone condition the candidates are temperature-
dependent realised variance (accounts for about a quarter of the trend, from the stored moments), and an
escape that at high k_BT_eff (σ up to 0.13 m/s) does not follow the minimum-energy path, e.g. lateral excursions
that see a lower or anharmonic barrier. The result does not support a ΔU-dependent prefactor; it does show that
the single-number summary "ratio ≈ 1 per condition" hides a decline of the alone ratio with depth.

## Paper-claim flags
- Sect. 5.2: "Per snapshot and condition the escape rate follows the Arrhenius law with that barrier, to within
  the fitted attempt rate." and "Its interval covers 1 in ... 9 of 10 (alone) fits." The per-fit statement stays
  true, but the alone ratios decline with ΔU (b = −0.16 [−0.26, −0.07] per unit ln ΔU, Spearman −0.89): the
  deepest alone barriers are crossed with 0.6–0.75 of the predicted slope. This should be stated.
- No claim about a pooled trend is contradicted; the pooled fit with condition intercepts shows none.
