# B6 Line goal for the pair standoff (referee m16)

**Done.** Scripts in experiments/revision: B6_static.py, B6_quench.py, B6_gen.py, B6_analyze.py ->
data/derived/rev_B6.json; force and checks in B5_lib.py. Raw data in data/revision/B6/: static, quench and gen_line
(.jsonl), snapshots in library/.

- Statics: the 46 pair snapshots of the E7b library, relaxed with the line-goal C1 rest force (v = 0, densities frozen,
  b = 0; pairlib.relax rules), Newton polish, then the B5 checks: −sym(J) positive definite, contact-free, x < 10,
  opposite sides, front of jambs.
- Quench: C1, CF ∪ {B_w: 0.04} ∪ {w2_goal: line}, Δt = 0.01 s, 120 s, deterministic stationary stop (the e7b_quench
  runner, unchanged); 46 unperturbed runs, 230 replays displaced by 1e-9 m (seeds 900–904), 46 point-goal controls.
- Generation: the 320 E7b library seeds (242–561), C1, w = 0.8 m, 100 agents, 600 s cap, flags CF ∪ {B_w: 0.04} ∪
  {w2_goal: line}, runner runlib.whole_run (unchanged), compared seed by seed with the point-goal runs of
  data/E7b/e7b_lib.jsonl. Counted per run: completion, deadlock, pair standoff (e7b_lib.qualifies), collective
  deadlock (the residual agent nearest (10, 5) has a negative stuck-alone margin), lone deadlock (a deadlock that is
  not collective), the share of deadlocks that are pair standoffs, and the overhang of each generated pair
  (min over members of |y − 5| − w/2). Exact McNemar test on each paired indicator, Wilson 95% intervals.

Line goal in the code (checked). With w2_goal = "line", Simulation.step replaces each agent's goal at every step by
the nearest point of the segment x = 11, y ∈ [5 − w/2 + r_i, 5 + w/2 − r_i] (BottleneckScenario.line_goals); it is
used for steering only. For a member at y ≈ 4.59 with r = 0.25 m the goal moves from (11, 5) to (11, ≈ 4.9), which
weakens the drive's push towards the axis. The drive c_i e_i, with e_i = −∇ dist(x_i, goal segment), is a gradient.
The static force evaluates line_goals at every call; its Jacobian asymmetry is ≤ 2.3e-10 relative.

CPU: static 0.3 CPU-min, quench 10.6 CPU-min (322 runs, one worker), generation 2.4 CPU-h, smoke tests ≈ 1 CPU-min.

**Result.**

*Generation.* Under the line goal pair standoffs form twice as often: 91 of 320 runs (0.28, 95% interval 0.24–0.34)
against 46 of 320 with the point goal (0.14, 0.11–0.19). Of the discordant seeds, 74 end in a pair standoff only
under the line goal and 29 only under the point goal (McNemar p = 1e-5). Collective deadlocks rise from 57 to 102
(0.18 to 0.32, interval 0.27–0.37; p = 2e-5), all deadlocks from 64 to 107 (p = 4e-5), and completion falls from 256
to 213 of 320 (0.80 to 0.67, interval 0.61–0.72). Lone deadlocks do not change measurably, 5 against 7 (p = 0.73).
Pair standoffs make up 0.85 (0.77–0.91) of the line-goal deadlocks and 0.72 (0.60–0.81) of the point-goal ones. The
generated pairs have the point-goal geometry: the inner member's centre lies 0.14–62 mm beyond the door edge (median
25 mm), against 0.26–59 mm (median 25 mm) with the point goal, and none lies more than 0.1 m beyond it.

Physically, the point goal at (11, 5) gives an off-axis member a drive component towards the axis, which pushes it off
the jamb face into the opening. The line goal steers each member to the nearest point of its passable band, so the
lateral component shrinks, and members that would slide off the face come to rest against it. The static result
agrees: every point-goal minimum persists, and its softest stiffness rises by 4–7%. The pair standoff is a property of
the jamb geometry and the drive towards the opening; a point goal is not needed for it.

*Statics.* All 46 snapshots remain strict minima, all contact-free and in front of the jambs; no relaxation frees a
member. The members move by 8–48 mm (median 22 mm). The softest stiffness μ1 = 69–128 N/m (median 100), against
65–121 N/m (median 94) with the point goal; the ratio line/point is 1.04–1.07.

*Quench.* All 46 unperturbed line-goal runs are held and stop stationary (Wilson lower bound 0.92), as are all 230
replays displaced by 1e-9 m (lower bound 0.98). The point-goal control is held in 46 of 46. Static and quench agree in
46 of 46.

*Earlier data.* E7's line-goal cell (default walls, B_w = 0.08 m) gave 3 of 97 classified deadlocks as collective,
against 2 of 98 with the point goal, and completion 3 of 100 against 2 of 100. That wall range produces few collective
deadlocks under either goal; the generation above, at B_w = 0.04 m, is the test of the pair standoff.

**Claims.** The paper's pair-standoff claim is strengthened. Referee m16 asked whether the standoff is an artefact of
the point goal; with a line goal every existing pair minimum persists, every quench holds, and fresh runs form pair
standoffs twice as often (91 against 46 of 320). Sect. 5.1's "a line goal … leaves the trap in place" holds for the
pair standoff as well, and it should add that the line goal raises the deadlock rate (64 to 107 of 320 at
B_w = 0.04 m). Together with B5: the standoff depends on the square jamb corner and survives a change of goal.
