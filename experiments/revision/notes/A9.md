# A9 Overlap J reconciliation

Script: `experiments/revision/A9_overlap_J.py` -> `data/derived/rev_A9.json`; registry `notes/A9.yaml` (prefix `RevAnine`). Existing data only, CPU a few seconds.

## Definitions

- **Unresolved under noise.** A snapshot freed in k of its m seeds is unresolved under one of three rules:
  - *half*: k < m/2 (5 seeds: k ≤ 2; 20 seeds: k ≤ 9). This is the rule already used by E2 `analyze.py`, `fix_a1.py` and `analyze_N1.py`.
  - *none*: k = 0.
  - *all*: k < m.
- **Unresolved under force-coupled ORCA.** The unperturbed run holds: the blocker is not out by 120 s, or the whole run is not complete at 600 s.
- **Whole runs.** There is one run per seed and stream. With the two σ* streams combined (E6b + N2, m = 2), *half* = *none* = fails in both, and *all* = fails in either.
- **Pairs.** Both arms are deterministic. Table 3 compares the minority (freed) sets; the held sets are given too.
- **Independence.** Set sizes a, b and universe N are fixed, and the overlap X is hypergeometric.
  - p = P(X ≥ x_obs) = Σ_{k≥x} C(a,k)C(N−a,b−k)/C(N,b), exact.
  - E X = ab/N, and "J at E X" = (ab/N)/(a+b−ab/N).
  - E J = Σ P(X=k)·k/(a+b−k). This is the value Table 3 prints in parentheses.

- Under *none*, almost no snapshot qualifies: at 20 seeds every lone keystone is freed at least once.
- Under *all*, the noise set holds every snapshot where seeds disagree. It then includes every ORCA-unresolved snapshot, but J is diluted by the 10–14 snapshots that ORCA frees.
- *half* is the only rule whose set size is comparable to ORCA's. It is also the published rule, and we keep it.

## Why Table 3 (J = 0.20) and the text (J = 0.75) differ

The two values come from the same set definition (half rule, per snapshot), the same universe (34 lone keystones) and the same ORCA set. That set is {seed83, seed87, seed203, seed211} and is identical for both solvers. Neither is per run; both are per snapshot. The noise levels differ only negligibly: 0.07 vs 0.0717 m/s, and the pooled freed fractions are 148/170 = 0.87 and 588/680 = 0.86.

The difference is the number of seeds behind the majority call: 5 (E2) against 20 (N1).

| snapshot | k/5 at σ = 0.07 | k/20 at σ = 0.0717 | ORCA (RVO2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| seed83 | 0 | 1 | holds |
| seed203 | 3 | 5 | holds |
| seed87 | 4 | 6 | holds |
| seed211 | 3 | 12 | holds |
| seed69 | 2 | 16 | frees |

- With five seeds, seed203 and seed87 are called resolved. The 20-seed probabilities are 0.25 and 0.30, so P(≥ 3 of 5) = 0.10 and 0.16.
- Seed69 is called unresolved with five seeds; its 20-seed probability is 0.80, so P(≤ 2 of 5) = 0.06.
- Given the N1 probabilities, a five-seed majority call misclassifies 1.5 snapshots on average.

Three checks quantify the five-seed spread:
- **Every 5-seed subset of the 20 N1 seeds** (15504 subsets): J has median 0.60, 95% range [0.33, 1.00], and J ≤ 0.20 in 0.8% of subsets.
- **Parametric five-seed replicates** (Binomial(5, k_i/20)): median J 0.60, P(J ≤ 0.20) = 0.007.
- **Exchangeability of the E2 and N1 seeds.** The per-snapshot outcomes of the two runs are consistent: the heterogeneity statistic gives permutation p = 0.48. Splitting the pooled 25 outcomes per snapshot into 5 + 20 at random gives P(J5 ≤ 0.20) = 0.025.
- **All 25 seeds pooled:** J = 0.75, with the same 3 of 4 shared.

So Table 3's 0.20 is a low-tail five-seed draw of the same quantity that N1 measures at 0.75.

**Claim-relevant.**
- Table 3 prints J = 0.20 [0.00, 0.70] (independence 0.05) for the lone keystone, which reads as noise and ORCA failing on different snapshots. The 20-seed data show the opposite: noise leaves unresolved 3 of ORCA's 4 unresolved snapshots and no other (J = 0.75, exact p = 6.7e−4 against independence).
- The text already says this ("as much as noise and ORCA do (J=\RunNOneJNoiseOrca …)"). Table and text are therefore inconsistent, and the table value is the noisier estimate.
- The second-trap J = 0.20 is also a five-seed value. Its p = 0.27 does not distinguish it from independence, and no 20-seed second-trap data exist.

**Recommendation.**
- Report the 20-seed N1 value in Table 3's lone-keystone row: J = 0.75 [0.00, 1.00], independence 0.06, exact hypergeometric p = 6.7e−4. Either move the σ = 0.0717 run into the row, or keep σ = 0.07 for the rates and take J from N1 with a footnote.
- Drop the five-seed J, or give it only with its five-seed spread (median 0.60, range [0.33, 1.00]).
- State the rule: "unresolved under noise: freed in fewer than half its seeds".
- Replace the permutation p-values with the exact hypergeometric ones in this note. They agree to within Monte Carlo error: 6.7e−4 vs 4.0e−4 for N1, 2.0e−3 vs 2.3e−3 for whole runs.
