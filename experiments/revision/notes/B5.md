# B5 Rounded jamb ends for the pair standoff; the barrier under bias

Scripts: experiments/revision/B5_lib.py (force, relaxation, checks), B5_static.py (phases reequil, cont, bisect,
barrier), B5_analyze.py -> data/derived/rev_B5.json. Raw: data/revision/B5/{reequil,cont,bisect,barrier_sharp}.jsonl.
Queued (not run): B5_quench.py, B5_gen.py.

## 0. How the code builds rounded edges (checked)

`BottleneckScenario(edge="rounded", edge_radius=r_c)` replaces each jamb by a capsule: segment x = 10 + r_c from the
outer wall to y = 5 ∓ (w/2 + r_c), radius r_c. The room-side face stays at x = 10, the door edge becomes a half circle,
the clear width stays w = 0.8 m. `agents_to_walls` returns d = (distance to the segment) − r_c, and WallForces applies
the same law A_w exp((r − d)/B_w) n with the run's B_w = 0.04 m on every wall (no B_w_edge). The CF corner rule
(convex_union) excludes capsules from its junction list; it acts only at the room corners, far from the door. The
force on the two members was evaluated on a two-agent copy of the state, identical to pairlib.code_force (max difference
0.0 N on 5 snapshots).

**Rounding does not make the wall force smooth.** The capsule distance is the segment distance minus r_c. It is C¹, but
its Hessian jumps from 0 on the flat face to (I − n nᵀ)/(d + r_c) on the cap. The wall force is therefore continuous and
its Jacobian jumps where the closest point moves from the face to the cap. This is the same kind of switch as at the end
of a sharp segment, and the jump is smaller only by the factor d/(d + r_c). The brief's expectation of a smooth
saddle-node therefore does not apply to this implementation. A smooth door edge would need a different wall law, such
as a smooth distance function.

## 1. Re-equilibration of the 46 pair snapshots with rounded edges (r_c = 0.1 m)

Rule: start the two members from the sharp-edge snapshot positions. Relax them as pairlib.relax does (dx/dt = (τ/m)F,
v = 0, densities frozen, Euler 0.01 s, up to 120 s; freed when a member reaches x > 10.3 m; held when max|F| < 0.05 N),
then polish with Newton (|F| < 1e-9 N). A minimum is kept if −sym(J) is positive definite, the pair is contact-free,
both members have x < 10, and they sit on opposite sides of the axis.

**Result: 0 of 46 survive** (RevBfiveKept of RevBfiveN). In all 46 the relaxation frees a member, after 1.7–5.1 s of
relaxation time (median 2.5 s). The first member out is the lower one in 24 cases and the upper one in 22. No snapshot
converges to another rest state, and none is undecided.

**Mechanism: an exact geometric rule.** In every sharp-edge snapshot the inner member's centre lies beyond the door
edge, behind the flat jamb face. Define the overhang as min_i |y_i − 5| − w/2. It ranges over 0.26–59 mm (median 25 mm;
RevBfiveOverhangMin/Med/Max). The closest wall point of each member is therefore on the flat face, where the wall force
is purely axial. Rounding with radius r_c moves the end of the flat face to |y − 5| = w/2 + r_c. A member whose
overhang is smaller than r_c then faces the cap. The cap's normal has a component towards the axis, about 17% of the
wall force at r_c = 0.1 m, roughly 28 N. That push drives the members into the opening, and the pair runs out.

We scanned r_c ∈ {0.1, 0.05, 0.02, 0.01, 0.003, 0.001} m. The kept counts are 0, 2, 27, 39, 44 and 45 of 46. The rule
"kept iff overhang > r_c" holds in all 276 (snapshot, r_c) cases, with 0 mismatches (RevBfiveRuleMismatch). The kept
minima do not move (max 6.4e-6 m), because their closest wall points stay on the flat face, where the rounded force
equals the sharp one.

Supporting count from earlier data: in E7's rounded-corner cell (default walls, B_w = 0.08 m), 0 of 93 classified
deadlocks are pair or group standoffs, against 2 of 98 with sharp corners (data/derived/E7_analysis.json). The line
goal combined with rounded corners gives 0 of 91.

## 2. Bisection and continuation to the loss with rounded edges

There is no minimum to continue at r_c = 0.1 m. So the continuation and bisection were run at r_c = 0.02 m, on the
27 snapshots that keep a minimum there (54 snapshot–sign cases), starting from the rounded minimum.

- **Continuation** (the runs3/N6 pseudo-arclength algorithm with fold refinement, plus natural-parameter continuation
  with step halving down to 1e-6): the fold is located in 27 of 54 cases. In the other 27 the pseudo-arclength
  corrector fails, as in N6. Natural continuation ends at a segment end in all 54 (the face/cap transition, closest
  point at t ≥ 1 − 1e-3). Natural continuation reaches 0.9992–1.0000 of b_f where both are available. At the fold, a
  member sits at the face/cap transition in 27 of 27 cases.
- **Exponents in δ = 1 − b/b_f** (log-log slope over δ ∈ {0.3, 0.1, 0.03, 0.01, 0.003, 0.001}), for the 27 fold cases:
  - minimum–saddle distance: median 1.00 (range 0.999–1.000), within 0.1 of 1 in 27 of 27 and within 0.1 of 0.5 in 0;
  - μ1 at the minimum: slope 5e-6 (|slope| < 0.01 in 27 of 27). μ1 stays constant up to the loss, with
    μ1(δ = 0.001)/μ1(δ = 0.3) = 0.9993–1.0000, and μ1 at the loss is 40–121 N/m over all 54 cases;
  - work along the straight segment: slope 2.00;
  - relative Jacobian jump between minimum and saddle at δ = 0.001: 0.059–0.075 (median 0.068).

  Sharp-edge reference (data/N6, rev_B5 barrier_sharp): distance exponent 1.00, μ1 slope 3e-5, W exponent 2.00,
  Jacobian jump 0.071. **The rounded loss is the same non-smooth border collision as the sharp one, with exponents
  1 (distance), 0 (μ1) and 2 (work).** The smooth saddle-node values would be 0.5, 0.5 and 1.5. This follows from §0:
  the Jacobian still jumps at the face/cap transition.
- **Bisection** (fold_threshold rules: grid 0–0.1, tolerance 1e-4, relaxing from the rounded minimum): all 54 cases
  decided, with no undecided relaxation. Lower-sign threshold b* = 0.0011–0.026 (median 0.0087).
  b*(r_c = 0.02)/b*(sharp) = 0.076–0.77 (median 0.49): rounding halves the threshold. b_f/b* = 0.70–0.99 (median 0.97);
  sharp: median 0.986. The first member out at the upper end of the bracket is lower in 27 cases and upper in 27.

## 3. The barrier under bias

**The biased rest field is not a gradient.** The tilt is c_i (e_i + b e⊥_i)/√(1+b²), where e⊥ is e rotated by +90°
and e points from the agent to g. The term c b e⊥/√(1+b²) is an azimuthal field about g. Its curl is
−c_i b/(√(1+b²) ρ_i), with ρ_i = |x_i − g|. A tilt along a fixed direction would be conservative; this one is not.
Numerically, curl_i = ∂F_yi/∂x_i − ∂F_xi/∂y_i from the Jacobian at the minimum matches this prediction to a ratio of
1.0000 (range 0.99999–1.00001) in all 790 (sharp) and 324 (r_c = 0.02) member–point evaluations. The cross-member
antisymmetry is at numerical zero (< 2e-10 relative), so the walls and the pair term stay conservative. The curl is
small against the soft stiffness: at δ = 0.001, |curl|/μ1 is a median of 0.028 (maximum 0.075) for sharp edges.

**Work along two paths.** W = −∫F·dX was computed from the minimum to the saddle along two paths. One is the straight
segment (N6's definition). The other is the saddle's unstable manifold, the heteroclinic flow line of dX/dt = M F with
M = diag(τ/m); this is the fixed point of the string method on a non-gradient field. On it, W_flow = ∫F·MF dt
(RK45, ended within 1e-3 of the distance from the minimum; it reached the minimum in 395 of 395 sharp cases and 162 of
162 rounded ones).

- Sharp edges (66 folds × 6 δ): |W_line − W_flow|/W_flow has median 0.0021. It is ≤ 1% in 391 of 395 cases. The 4
  larger values occur where W < 1e-8 J, at the integration noise floor.
- Exponents: W_line 2.00 and W_flow 2.00 (median; range 2.00–2.21). r_c = 0.02: median 0.0025, ≤ 1% in 157 of 162,
  exponent 2.00.
- Scale: W_flow at δ = 0.3 is a median 0.021 J (range 1e-6 to 0.12 J) for sharp edges.

**What to report and why.** The barrier is path dependent, but only at the 0.2% level, because the circulation per
unit area (the curl) is about 3% of the soft stiffness. We propose to keep the exponent and define the barrier as the
work against the rest field along the heteroclinic path from saddle to minimum, i.e. the string-method path. The paper
should state that the field is not a gradient and give the path dependence (median 0.2% against the straight segment).
The exponent, 2.00, is the same on both paths. This work is not a quasi-potential, and no Arrhenius claim rests on it
(the bias runs are deterministic).

## Claims this changes or sharpens (Sect. 5.3)

- "Two agents stand before the jamb faces …" → each inner member's centre lies 0.3–59 mm beyond the door edge,
  behind the flat face. The standoff exists only with the jamb's square corner. With rounded jamb ends of radius
  r_c it survives exactly when this overhang exceeds r_c: none of 46 at r_c = 0.1 m, 27 at 0.02 m.
- Sect. 5.1: "Rounded jamb corners, a line goal or both leave the trap in place" remains true for lone traps (E7).
  It should not be read as covering the pair standoff, which rounding removes.
- "the barrier between them with exponent \RunNSixWSlopeMed" → the field under bias is not a gradient. Define the
  barrier as the work along the heteroclinic path (string method); its path dependence is 0.2%.
- The non-smooth loss is not an artefact of zero-thickness jambs: rounded (capsule) jambs give the same exponents,
  because the capsule wall force also has a Jacobian jump at the face/cap transition.
