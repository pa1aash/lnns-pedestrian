How the code builds rounded edges (checked). `BottleneckScenario(edge="rounded", edge_radius=r_c)` replaces each jamb
by a capsule: the segment x = 10 + r_c from the outer wall to y = 5 ∓ (w/2 + r_c), with radius r_c. The room-side
face stays at x = 10, the door edge becomes a half circle, and the clear width stays w = 0.8 m. `agents_to_walls`
returns d = (distance to the segment) − r_c, and WallForces applies the same law A_w exp((r − d)/B_w) n with the run's
B_w = 0.04 m on every wall (no B_w_edge). The CF corner rule (convex_union) excludes capsules from its junction list
and acts only at the room corners, far from the door. On a two-agent copy of the state the force on the two members
equals pairlib.code_force (largest difference 0.0 N on 5 snapshots).

The capsule wall force has a Jacobian jump. The capsule distance is the segment distance minus r_c; it is C¹, and its
Hessian jumps from 0 on the flat face to (I − n nᵀ)/(d + r_c) on the cap. The wall force is continuous and its
Jacobian jumps where the closest point moves from the face to the cap, the same kind of switch as at the end of a
sharp segment, smaller by the factor d/(d + r_c). The smooth saddle-node expected in the brief needs a smooth wall
law, such as a smooth distance function; this implementation does not provide one.

CPU: statics ≈ 27 CPU-min (reequil 0.5, cont 12.3, bisect 4.6, barrier 7.9, smoke tests and exploration ≈ 2);
quench 0.14 CPU-h; generation 1.8 CPU-h.

**Result.**

*Generation.* With rounded jamb ends of radius 0.1 m no pair standoff forms: 0 of 320 runs (Wilson upper bound
0.012), against 46 of 320 with sharp jambs (0.14, 95% interval 0.11–0.19; McNemar p = 3e-14, all 46 discordant pairs
on the sharp side). Collective deadlocks vanish as well, 0 against 57 (0.18, 0.14–0.22; p = 1e-17). Completion rises
from 256 to 319 of 320 (0.80 to 0.997, interval 0.983–0.999). The one remaining deadlock (seed 269) is a lone trap: a
single agent at rest on the axis at x = 9.88 m with stuck-alone margin +27 N. Lone deadlocks fall from 7 to 1
(p = 0.031). The generated pair standoffs have overhang 0.26–59 mm with sharp jambs and 0.14–62 mm under the line goal
(B6), all below 0.1 m, so the static rule below predicts that none survives r_c = 0.1 m; the fresh runs also form no
pair at a larger overhang. Physically, the pair standoff needs the flat jamb face to reach the door edge
to within the inner member's overhang. It is a property of square-cornered jambs. Generation was run at r_c = 0.1 m
only; at 0.02 and 0.01 m the static scan keeps 27 and 39 of 46 minima.

*Quench at b = 0, r_c = 0.1 m.* All 46 runs free a member (Wilson lower bound 0.92), and in all 46 both members exit.
The first exit comes after 3.1–7.5 s (median 4.6 s); no run stops stationary. This is the dynamic counterpart of the
static loss below.

*Quench just past the fold, r_c = 0.02 m.* All 216 runs with b = s b_f (1 + ε) free a member (lower bound 0.98), for
both sources of b_f. The passage time is 7.6–28 s. Its median falls from 19 s at ε = 0.01 to 10 s at ε = 0.3, a
log-log slope of the medians of −0.19; a smooth saddle-node bottleneck would give −0.5.

*Quench on the bias grid, r_c = 0.02 m.* Quench and static rule agree in 213 of 216 runs (0.96–0.995). No run is held
above b*. Three runs are freed below b*, by at most 3.4% of b* (b = 0.02 at b* = 0.0201; b = 0.01 at b* = 0.0104,
twice), all with the lower member first. The bisection relaxes without inertia (dX/dt = (τ/m)F); the quench integrates
the velocities, and an overshoot on the approach to the biased rest state is a candidate cause that we did not test.

*Re-equilibration with rounded edges, r_c = 0.1 m.* The two members start from the sharp-edge snapshot positions and
relax as in pairlib.relax (dX/dt = (τ/m)F, v = 0, densities frozen, Euler 0.01 s, up to 120 s; freed when a member
reaches x > 10.3 m; held when max|F| < 0.05 N), followed by a Newton polish (|F| < 1e-9 N). A minimum is kept if
−sym(J) is positive definite, the pair is contact-free, both members have x < 10 and they sit on opposite sides of the
axis. None of 46 survives. In all 46 the relaxation frees a member, after 1.7–5.1 s of relaxation time (median
2.5 s); the lower member leaves first in 24 cases and the upper in 22. No snapshot converges to another rest state,
and none is undecided.

*The geometric rule.* In every sharp-edge snapshot the inner member's centre lies beyond the door edge, behind the
flat jamb face. Define the overhang as min_i |y_i − 5| − w/2; it ranges over 0.26–59 mm (median 25 mm). The closest
wall point of each member is on the flat face, where the wall force is axial. Rounding with radius r_c moves the end
of the flat face to |y − 5| = w/2 + r_c, so a member whose overhang is smaller than r_c faces the cap. The cap's
normal has a component towards the axis, ≈ 17% of the wall force at r_c = 0.1 m (≈ 28 N), which drives the members
into the opening. Over r_c ∈ {0.1, 0.05, 0.02, 0.01, 0.003, 0.001} m the kept counts are 0, 2, 27, 39, 44 and 45 of
46. The rule "kept iff overhang > r_c" holds in all 276 (snapshot, r_c) cases, with 0 mismatches. The kept minima
move by at most 6.4e-6 m, because their closest wall points stay on the flat face, where the rounded force equals the
sharp one. From earlier data: E7's rounded-corner cell (default walls, B_w = 0.08 m) has 0 of 93 classified deadlocks
as pair or group standoffs, against 2 of 98 with sharp corners (data/derived/E7_analysis.json); line goal with rounded
corners gives 0 of 91.

*Continuation and bisection, r_c = 0.02 m.* At r_c = 0.1 m there is no minimum to continue, so these ran at 0.02 m on
the 27 snapshots that keep a minimum there (54 snapshot–sign cases), from the rounded minimum.

- Continuation (the runs3/N6 pseudo-arclength algorithm with fold refinement, plus natural-parameter continuation
  with step halving down to 1e-6) locates the fold in 27 of 54 cases; in the other 27 the pseudo-arclength corrector
  fails, as in N6. Natural continuation ends at a segment end in all 54 (the face/cap transition, closest point at
  t ≥ 1 − 1e-3) and reaches 0.9992–1.0000 of b_f where both are available. At the fold a member sits at the face/cap
  transition in 27 of 27 cases.
- Exponents in δ = 1 − b/b_f (log-log slope over δ ∈ {0.3, 0.1, 0.03, 0.01, 0.003, 0.001}, 27 fold cases):
  minimum–saddle distance, median 1.00 (0.999–1.000), within 0.1 of 1 in 27 of 27 and within 0.1 of 0.5 in 0; μ1 at
  the minimum, slope 5e-6 (|slope| < 0.01 in 27 of 27), with μ1(δ = 0.001)/μ1(δ = 0.3) = 0.9993–1.0000 and μ1 at the
  loss 40–121 N/m over all 54 cases; work along the straight segment, slope 2.00; relative Jacobian jump between
  minimum and saddle at δ = 0.001, 0.059–0.075 (median 0.068).
- Sharp-edge reference (data/N6, rev_B5 barrier_sharp): distance exponent 1.00, μ1 slope 3e-5, work exponent 2.00,
  Jacobian jump 0.071. The rounded loss is the same non-smooth border collision as the sharp one, with exponents 1
  (distance), 0 (μ1) and 2 (work); a smooth saddle-node gives 0.5, 0.5 and 1.5. The cause is the Jacobian jump at the
  face/cap transition.
- Bisection (fold_threshold rules: grid 0–0.1, tolerance 1e-4, relaxation from the rounded minimum) decides all 54
  cases, with no undecided relaxation. Lower-sign threshold b* = 0.0011–0.026 (median 0.0087).
  b*(r_c = 0.02)/b*(sharp) = 0.076–0.77 (median 0.49): rounding halves the threshold. b_f/b* = 0.70–0.99 (median
  0.97); sharp, median 0.986. At the upper end of the bracket the lower member leaves first in 27 cases and the upper
  in 27.

*The biased rest field is not a gradient.* The tilt is c_i (e_i + b e⊥_i)/√(1+b²), where e⊥ is e rotated by +90° and
e points from the agent to g. The term c b e⊥/√(1+b²) is an azimuthal field about g with curl −c_i b/(√(1+b²) ρ_i),
ρ_i = |x_i − g|. A tilt along a fixed direction would be conservative. The measured curl_i = ∂F_yi/∂x_i − ∂F_xi/∂y_i
from the Jacobian at the minimum matches the prediction to a ratio of 1.0000 (0.99999–1.00001) in all 790 (sharp) and
324 (r_c = 0.02) member–point evaluations. The cross-member antisymmetry is at numerical zero (< 2e-10 relative), so
the walls and the pair term stay conservative. At δ = 0.001, |curl|/μ1 has median 0.028 (largest 0.075) for sharp
edges.

*Work along two paths.* W = −∫F·dX from the minimum to the saddle was computed along the straight segment (N6's
definition) and along the saddle's unstable manifold, the heteroclinic flow line of dX/dt = M F with M = diag(τ/m),
which is the fixed point of the string method on a non-gradient field. On it W_flow = ∫F·MF dt (RK45, ended within
1e-3 of the distance from the minimum; it reached the minimum in 395 of 395 sharp cases and 162 of 162 rounded ones).
For sharp edges (66 folds × 6 δ), |W_line − W_flow|/W_flow has median 0.0021 and is ≤ 1% in 391 of 395 cases; the 4
larger values have W < 1e-8 J, at the integration noise floor. Exponents: W_line 2.00, W_flow 2.00 (median; range
2.00–2.21). At r_c = 0.02: median 0.0025, ≤ 1% in 157 of 162, exponent 2.00. W_flow at δ = 0.3 has median 0.021 J
(1e-6 to 0.12 J) for sharp edges.

*Barrier to report.* The path dependence is 0.2%, because the curl is 3% of the soft stiffness. We propose to keep
the exponent and define the barrier as the work against the rest field along the heteroclinic path from saddle to
minimum, the string-method path. The paper should state that the field is not a gradient and give the path
dependence (median 0.2% against the straight segment). The exponent is 2.00 on both paths. This work is not a
quasi-potential, and no Arrhenius claim rests on it (the bias runs are deterministic).

**Claims this changes or sharpens (Sect. 5.3, 5.1).**

- "Two agents stand before the jamb faces …" → each inner member's centre lies 0.26–59 mm beyond the door edge, behind
  the flat face. The standoff needs the jamb's square corner: with rounded jamb ends of radius r_c a minimum survives
  exactly when the overhang exceeds r_c (none of 46 at r_c = 0.1 m, 27 at 0.02 m), and in 320 fresh runs at
  r_c = 0.1 m no pair standoff and no collective deadlock forms (46 and 57 with sharp jambs).
- Sect. 5.1, "Rounded jamb corners, a line goal or both leave the trap in place", holds for lone traps in E7 (default
  walls, B_w = 0.08 m). It does not cover the pair standoff, which rounding removes. With B_w = 0.04 m, rounding at
  0.1 m also reduces lone deadlocks from 7 to 1 of 320 (p = 0.031); E7's statement should name its wall range.
- "the barrier between them with exponent \RunNSixWSlopeMed" → the field under bias is not a gradient; define the
  barrier as the work along the heteroclinic path (string method), whose path dependence is 0.2%.
- The non-smooth loss does not depend on zero-thickness jambs: capsule jambs give the same exponents, because the
  capsule wall force also has a Jacobian jump at the face/cap transition. Quenches confirm the static picture: at
  r_c = 0.1 m every pair leaves at b = 0, every run 1–30% past the fold leaves, and quench and static threshold agree
  in 213 of 216 grid runs.
