# B2 Critical door width, densely sampled, and the parameter-free prediction

**Done.** experiments/revision/B2_width.py: SFM C1, CF flags, default walls, 100 agents, seeds 42–141 (the E7 spawns),
w = 0.82–0.96 m in steps of 0.02 m, 600 s cap, 800 runs in data/revision/B2/b2.jsonl. The recorder is whole_run's,
plus three counts at each agent's door arrival (x > 9.4 m, |y − 5| < 0.6 m): agents still active (n_active), other
active agents with surface gap below 0.1 m (n_touch) and with centre within 1.0 m (n_1m). Every run ended by deadlock
or completion; none reached the cap. The 100 runs at w = 0.90 m repeat E7's w0.9 cell and reproduce it exactly (100 of
100 identical exit times), so experiments/revision/B2_analyze.py counts them once. The fit then uses 1100 runs, 100
per width at w = 0.70, 0.80, 0.82, …, 0.96 and 1.00 m (E7, E1b, B2), and writes data/derived/rev_B2.json. The
completion curve is a binomial logit fitted by maximum likelihood, with w50 = −a/b and a 95% interval from 2000
bootstrap resamples of runs within each width. f(w) is the fraction of the 10 000 spawned agents whose stuck-alone
width w_c (margin zero, fix_a8.w_crit) exceeds w. The prediction is completion = (1 − f(w))^N. N = 100 is the
parameter-free case: no agent may be stuck alone. N_eff comes from the arrival record. It is the n_active at which a
stuck-alone agent arriving at the door passes with probability 1/2 (logit in n_active over 5676 stuck-alone arrivals
at w ≥ 0.8 m; 95% interval from 1000 bootstrap resamples of seeds). N_eff,q = 100 q, where q is the fraction of
stuck-alone arrivals that do not pass. N_best is the N that maximises the likelihood of the observed counts; it is a
fit, reported as a check.

**Result.** Completed runs per 100: 0 (0.70 m), 2 (0.80), 6 (0.82), 15 (0.84), 42 (0.86), 52 (0.88), 70 (0.90), 82
(0.92), 93 (0.94), 96 (0.96), 100 (1.00). w50 = 0.878 m [0.874, 0.883]; slope 42 per m, so completion rises from 0.1
to 0.9 over 0.11 m. The four E7/E1b widths alone gave 0.882 [0.873, 0.897]; the new widths narrow the interval
from 0.024 m to 0.009 m and move w50 by 0.004 m. The bracket holds: w_lo = 0.75 m (median agent w_c) and w_hi = 0.92 m (median over
runs of the largest w_c). **The parameter-free prediction is refuted.** N = 100 puts w50 at 0.919 m, 0.041 m above
the observed value and outside its interval. At w = 0.88 m it predicts 0.06 completion, against 0.52 observed, with
f(0.88) = 0.028. Its −2 ln L exceeds the logistic fit's by 1069. The arrival record shows why most stuck-alone agents
do not block. Of the 5676 stuck-alone arrivals, 5109 pass. All 3186 that arrive with 21 or more agents active pass,
737 of 739 with 11–20, 756 of 798 with 6–10, 424 of 768 with 3–5, and 6 of 185 with 1–2. In the B2 runs, the 388
stuck-alone agents held at the door arrived with at most 9 agents active (median 3). None had another agent within
0.1 m of its surface, and the median count within 1 m was 0 (max 4). The 2922 that passed arrived with a median of
34 active and a median of 5 agents within 1 m. In 313 of the 314 deadlocked runs a stuck-alone agent is held at the
door as the blocker. The N taken from the arrival record is too small. N_eff = 3.7 [3.6, 3.9] predicts w50 = 0.816 m (0.062 m
low; Δ(−2 ln L) = 603), and N_eff,q = 10 (q = 0.10) predicts 0.852 m (0.026 m low; Δ = 115). The best-fitting N is
19, with w50 = 0.873 m and Δ = 5.5. The observed curve lies between N = 100 and the counts from the record. The
crowd pushes stuck-alone agents through, so N = 100 overcounts the agents that can block, and the last few arrivals
undercount them.

**Draft sentence.** Half the runs complete at w50 = 0.878 m [0.874, 0.883] (100 runs at each of 11 widths), 0.041 m
below the 0.919 m predicted by requiring that none of the 100 agents be stuck alone, because the crowd pushes
stuck-alone agents through the door and only those arriving with at most 9 agents still active are held.
