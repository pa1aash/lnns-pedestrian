## 6. Arch compression: per contact or summed?
Source: experiments/arch/arch.py:58-64 (`load = K * sum(max(ov,0)) / 2` over the arch component's contact matrix, K = 1.2e5 kg/s²); N5.py reuses `arch.arch_check` unchanged; analyze.py:63 and analyze_N5.py:50 take the maximum over episodes. The stored quantity is **summed over all agent-agent contacts of the arch** and counts only the linear contact term k(r_ij − d_ij). The exponential term A e^{(r−d)/B} and the jamb contacts are left out.
The maximum single-contact overlap is not stored, so I recomputed it from the peak checks (bit-identical re-runs):
- 108 kN (seed 61, Δt = 0.01 s, t = 1.4 s, a single 0.1 s check during the initial rush): 9 agents, 10 contacts, summed overlap 0.90 m. Largest single overlap **0.24 m** (radii 0.20–0.28 m: almost one radius). Single-contact force k·ov + A e^{ov/B} = **67 kN**. Largest jamb overlap 0.09 m.
- 22.2 kN (seed 60, Δt = 0.0025 s, t = 1.9 s): 12 agents, 11 contacts, summed overlap 0.18 m. Largest single overlap **3.2 cm**, single-contact force 6.9 kN.

**Flag.** "Carry up to 108 kN of body compression" reads as a force on a body. It is a sum over 10 contacts, and it is dominated by an unphysical 24 cm interpenetration at Δt = 0.01 s, where ω Δt = sqrt(k/μ) Δt ≈ 0.55 for the reduced mass μ = 40 kg, so the contact is under-resolved. The value falls fivefold at Δt = 0.0025 s.
Draft: "…carry a summed contact compression $k\sum(r_{ij}-d_{ij})$ of up to \ArchPeakForce\,kN, a transient of one 0.1\,s check with single overlaps up to \RevAthirteenArchMaxOverlap\,cm at $\Delta t=0.01$\,s; at $\Delta t=0.0025$\,s the peak is \RunNFiveFinePeakForce\,kN with single overlaps up to \RevAthirteenArchFineMaxOverlap\,cm."

## 9. Table 3 "Lower" column
Source: experiments/E7b/e7b_quench.py:61-62 (side = "lower" if the member's y < 5 m at the snapshot), analyze.py:15-17,248. The door axis is y = 5 m. The lower member is the one with y < 5, which is on the walker's right when facing the door (+x). The bias tilts ê by b ê⊥ with ê⊥ = (−e_y, e_x) (code/sim/steering/hybrid.py:151-153), which is +y for ê = +x. So b > 0 pushes the lower member towards the axis and favours it, and b < 0 favours the upper member. The column counts freed standoffs in which the lower member crosses the door first.
Draft (caption): "first exits by the lower member (the member at $y<5$\,m, on the right of the walking direction, favoured by $b>0$)".

## 13. Notation s(·;·,·) and N(·,·)
- s(x; x₀, c) = 1/(1 + e^{−c(x−x₀)}) (paper Sect. 3.2; code hybrid.py:130-134, 291-293; w2lib.py:123). In s(ρ; 4, 2) the second argument is the **midpoint** x₀ = 4 ped/m² and the third is the **steepness** c = 2 m²/ped. The paper's definition matches the code.
- 𝒩(μ, σ) in Table 1: the second argument is the **standard deviation** (code/sim/core/agent.py:96-97, `rng.normal(mean, std)`; model.yaml:16-17), giving v0 sd 0.26 m/s and r sd 0.03 m. Table 1 should say so. The lower bounds are clipping, `np.maximum(·, 0.5)`, so they leave point masses at 0.5 m/s and 0.15 m. They are not truncation or redrawing, although model.yaml's "redrawn per agent" could be misread.
Draft (Table 1): "$v_0\sim\mathcal N(1.34,\,0.26^2)$\,m/s clipped below at 0.5\,m/s; $r\sim\mathcal N(0.25,\,0.03^2)$\,m clipped below at 0.15\,m" (or keep $\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma)$ and state "second argument: standard deviation").
