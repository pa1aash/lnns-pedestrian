# A10: full-space Hessians at the 60 lone-trap snapshots

Script: `experiments/revision/A10_hessian.py` -> `data/derived/rev_A10.json` (registry `notes/A10.yaml`, prefix `RevAten`). CPU about 3 s; no dynamics.

## What was done
- Snapshots: the E2 library (`selection.json` "E2"): 34 lone keystones and 26 second-trap snapshots, all residual agents active (n = 1–9).
- Rest field: the code's own force at zero velocity, F0(x) = `HybridSteeringModel.compute_forces` under C1 with the CF flags (`w2lib.restore`, as every E2 quench). Neighbour lists (3 m) and grid densities are frozen at their snapshot values, and `CLIP_MODE = "none"` (fixed numerics). With the densities frozen, F0 = −∇U of Eq. (U).
- Hessian H = −∂F0/∂x in all 2n coordinates, from central differences of F0 at h = 1e-5 m. The step was checked by halving it to 5e-6 m. Asymmetry is measured as ‖H − Hᵀ‖_F/‖H‖_F before symmetrising. As an independent check, the result is compared with the finite-difference Hessian of the separately coded potential U of `experiments/BAR/bar.py`.
- Per snapshot the script reports: the residual max|F0|, the eigenvalues, whether the snapshot is a strict minimum, and the softest eigenvector. For that eigenvector it gives per-agent weights p_a = |e_a|², the blocker's weight, the participation ratio and a mode type.

## Results
- **Numerics.** The snapshots are rest states to max|F0| ≤ 6.1e-4 N (median 1.6e-4 N; snapshot speeds ≤ 2.2e-6 m/s). The relative asymmetry is ≤ 8.3e-10. Halving the step changes H by ≤ 2.0e-9 relative. H agrees with the bar.py Hessian of U to ≤ 5.3e-7 relative, so the code's rest field is the gradient of Eq. (U) to finite-difference accuracy.
- **All 60 snapshots are strict local minima** (positive definite: 60/60). No snapshot is a saddle and none has a flat direction.
- **Softest eigenvalue μ1:**
  - All 60: 66–1115 N/m, median 418.
  - Lone keystones: 239–1115, median 658.
  - Second traps: 66–424, median 182.
- **Lone blocker (n = 1, 24 snapshots).** The softest mode is the axial one in 23/24 (median 801 N/m, against 1824 N/m for the stiffer mode). This reproduces the keystone-test numbers (FinAxStiffMed 903, FinLatStiffMed 1723, from the K1 final positions). The exception, E1b_seed83 (μ1 = 869 N/m), is softer sideways.
- **Who carries the softest mode:**
  - Lone keystones: the blocker carries it in 27/34 overall, but only in 3 of the 10 that have neighbours. In the other 7 it is a common lateral shift of neighbours standing behind the blocker against the jambs.
  - Second traps: the blocker carries it in only 3/26 (median blocker weight 0.36). It is a common lateral shift in 19/26.
- **Second trap with one neighbour abreast.** In 9 snapshots (E1b_seed46, 60, 75, 111, 130, 209, 223, 230, 237) the blocker and its neighbour stand across the axis at the same x. Their softest mode is the common lateral shift with weights 0.49/0.51, μ1 = 85–120 N/m (median 94), and the opposed mode is stiffer by 12.1–12.9 times. These numbers match the pair library (PairLamOne 65–121 N/m, median 94, ratio 12–14). These second traps are therefore pair standoffs held under the default wall range.
- **Softest single value, E1b_seed172 (66 N/m).** This is a lateral mode of an agent 1.7 m behind the door (x = 8.28 m) in a 9-agent cluster. Its lateral stiffness is mainly the drive curvature c/|x − g| plus weak neighbour repulsion. This is physical: an agent behind the crowd is laterally soft.

## Paper impact
This confirms, and does not change, "every standoff is a strict local minimum" (Sect. 5.3). It extends the Hessian statement from the 4-coordinate pair and the blocker-alone stiffness to the full residual configuration of the lone traps. No paper claim changes.
