## Interpretation
At the lone-trap matched level (0.07 and 0.0717 m/s) the noise is not in the activated regime: k_BT_eff
(≈ 10 J) exceeds the median barrier (6.4 J), two thirds of the barriers have β < 1, and none has β ≥ 5. Second
traps are hotter still (median β ≈ 0.2). Kramers' asymptotics (β ≫ 1) apply to essentially none of these
barriers; the E4 Arrhenius test spans β = 1.5–3.5, and only 14 (0.07) or 15 (0.0717) of the 56 barriers lie in
that tested range, 2 and 1 above it. At σ* the median β is 1.4, 41% are below 1 and 7 of 56 reach β ≥ 5:
whole-run noise is at the edge of the activated regime, activated for the deeper lone keystones only.
The noise arm's class (Heat) therefore rests on its probe responses (replay sensitivity, time step, fixed point
left in place), not on Kramers' β ≫ 1 limit; at the fraction-matched levels most barriers are crossed in about
one attempt time.

## Paper-claim flags
- Sect. 4 Table 2 / Sect. 4.1 define Heat as carrying the state "over a barrier ΔU at a rate set by
  ΔU/k_BT_eff". At the fraction-matched level this description holds as an Arrhenius-type rate only for the
  minority of barriers with β ≳ 1.5; it is not contradicted, but the matched noise mostly acts with β < 1.
- Sect. 6.2: "The survival function of noise stalls lies within \EsixStallMaxDiff of its prediction from the
  \EsixBarrierN library barriers, k_BT_eff(σ*) and the attempt rate of Sect. 5.2, with no parameter fitted."
  The prediction uses ν e^{-β} for all 56 barriers, 23 of which have β < 1 and 28 of which lie below the
  E4-tested range (β < 1.5). The agreement is an extrapolation of the Arrhenius law below its tested range and
  should say so.
- Sect. 6.1.3: the paper already states that at β = 1.25 "Kramers' prefactor is not constant"; by the same
  standard the matched noise level σ = 0.07 m/s (median β 0.64) lies outside the Kramers regime.
